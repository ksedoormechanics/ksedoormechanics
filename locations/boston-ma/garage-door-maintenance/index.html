Signs it may be time to replace your garage door

Rust and corrosion

Road salt, slush and coastal humidity attack steel panels and hardware.

Cracked or sagging panels

Panels that flex or separate are a structural concern.

Frequent repairs

Repeated visits for the same door add up.

Drafts and cold garage

A poorly sealed or uninsulated door lets winter in, including to adjoining rooms.

Frozen or stiff operation

Ice at the threshold or a stiff seal can mimic a bigger fault. Diagnose first.

Door is noisy or shakes

Could be worn hardware, not necessarily the door.

Safety concerns

A door that falls, jams or fails to reverse needs urgent professional attention.

Appearance and curb appeal

A valid reason on its own.

What does a garage door replacement include?

Scope varies by company, so confirm it in writing. It typically covers removal of the old door, a new door and weather seal, matching springs and hardware as needed, installation, opener checks and testing. Haul-away, new tracks or a new opener may be separate items.

Should I repair or replace my garage door?

Repair often makes sense for a single worn part such as a spring, cable or roller. Replacement is worth considering when several panels are damaged, the door is badly rusted or warped, parts are hard to match, or repair costs approach a new door. An inspection will tell you which.

Can you replace just one garage door panel?

Sometimes. Whether a single panel or section can be replaced depends on the door model and whether a matching part is available. If not, the whole door may need replacing. We assess this during inspection.

What size is a standard garage door?

Single doors are commonly 8 or 9 feet wide, and double doors commonly 16 feet wide, with heights of 7 or 8 feet. Many older Boston garages are not standard, so measure the opening or let a technician measure it.

Which garage door is best for New England winters?

Many homeowners choose an insulated steel door with a protective finish and a good bottom seal. Compare R-values using the manufacturer's published ratings on the same basis. Wood and glass styles can work but need more care. The right choice depends on your garage use and budget.

Does a new garage door need a new opener?

Not always. A compatible opener in good condition can often be reused, but it must be checked, reset and tested with the new door. An older opener, or a heavier or lighter door, may justify replacing it. We assess both together.

Do I need a permit to replace a garage door in Boston?

How long does garage door replacement take?

Is a new garage door tested after installation?

It should be. After installation the door's balance, travel, safety sensors and automatic reversal should be checked, following the manufacturer's procedure. A heavier or lighter door can change balance, so spring selection matters.
