Safety First

Until a professional has assessed the door

  • Keep people and pets away from an unstable door.
  • Do not run a crooked or off-track door.
  • Do not touch broken springs, cables or other high-tension hardware.
  • Do not try to lift a heavy or unstable door by hand.
  • Do not adjust torsion springs, cables or drums.

If a door presents an immediate threat to people or property, keep clear and contact emergency services (911) if necessary. An opener’s manual-release feature should not be used with a broken spring, damaged cable or off-track door; if unsure, leave it alone.

Garage Door Emergency FAQs

What garage door problems may require urgent professional attention?

A door that is stuck open or closed, off its tracks, crooked or unstable, a spring that looks broken, a damaged or loose cable, a door that will not close securely, or sudden loud noises or movement. The right fix depends on the diagnosis, and not every situation is dangerous.

What should I do if my garage door is stuck open?

Keep people and vehicles clear and do not force it down. If it will not close securely, consider the safety of the property. Do not touch springs or cables. Describe what you see when you contact us so the cause can be narrowed down.

Is it safe to use a garage door with a broken spring?

No. A broken spring can leave the door very heavy and hard to control, and the hardware stays under tension. Do not operate it or lift it by hand. Keep clear and have it assessed by a professional.

Can I close a garage door that is off its tracks?

Do not try. A door with rollers outside the tracks can be unstable and may fall or move unexpectedly. Keep people and pets away, do not use the opener, and contact a professional.

Is a broken garage door considered an emergency?

It can be, mainly when the door is unstable, will not close and leaves a space unsecured, or traps a vehicle. Many problems, such as a worn roller or a noisy opener, are urgent only in the sense that they should not be ignored.
