Garage Door Opener Problems We Diagnose in Boston

Garage door opener not working

No power, a tripped breaker, a failed control board, or a door problem. Check for lights and sounds first.

Remote control not responding

Weak battery, lost programming, or a compatibility issue. Try the wall button.

Wall button malfunction

Power, lock mode, a damaged wall control, or a wiring fault.

Opener motor problems

Humming, clicking, or silence can point to the motor, gear, or board. A heavy door can also cause it.

Door reversing unexpectedly

Sensors, travel limits, or resistance in the door. Winter ice can trigger it.

Unusual noises

Worn chain, belt, trolley, or rollers, or dry hardware.

Door opening partially

A spring or balance problem, binding, or opener limits.

Safety sensor problems

Blocked, dirty, misaligned, or damaged sensors. They mostly affect closing.

Intermittent operation

Interference, a weak battery, a loose connection, or a failing part.

Each symptom has more than one possible cause and needs proper diagnosis.

Garage Door Opener Systems

Opener type affects what can go wrong and what parts are needed. Compatibility depends on manufacturer, model, door weight, configuration, and available replacement parts. We do not claim to support every model, so call with your brand and model.

  • Screw-drive: fewer moving parts; call to confirm model support.

  • Commercial operators: where supported. Call to confirm.

Is It the Opener or the Door?

When the motor runs but the door stays put, the opener is not always the failed part. The carriage may be disconnected, a drive gear or belt may have failed, or a broken spring may leave the door too heavy to lift. Do not try to reconnect the opener or lift the door by hand if it feels heavy or looks crooked. See garage door not opening and spring replacement.

Remote, Keypad and Wall Control Troubleshooting

A dead remote does not mean the opener is broken. We check remote batteries, programming, receiver response, wall control and lock mode, outside keypads, and power. Older openers and newer remotes or in-car controls may not be compatible, which is why the opener's age and model matter. Sensors are a separate system that mostly affects closing. See remote programming, keypad programming, and sensor repair.

Why We Don't Tell You to Adjust Force or Travel Limits

Many online guides suggest turning the opener's force or travel-limit settings to fix reversing or partial opening. We don't, because those settings can hide a real problem, such as a worn spring or a binding roller, and wrong settings can weaken the door's entrapment protection. A door that reverses or stops partway needs a diagnosis.

  • Do not bypass or disable safety sensors.
  • Do not force a door that is frozen, heavy, or crooked.
  • Do not open the opener housing or touch wiring.
  • If you smell burning or see smoke, stay clear and call a qualified professional.

Boston Garage Door Opener FAQs

Do you provide garage door opener repair in Boston?

Yes. KSE Door Mechanics provides garage door opener diagnosis and repair for Boston, Massachusetts. Our office is in Needham, MA, and Boston is our service area, not a Boston office. Call (833) 877-2561 to confirm your address, the opener model, and scheduling.

Why is my garage door opener not working?

Common causes are no power, a weak remote battery, a wall control or lock-mode issue, a failed drive part or board, a blocked sensor, or a mechanical problem with the door itself, such as a broken spring. What you hear and see narrows it down, but it takes an inspection to confirm.

Can you repair garage door opener remotes?

We troubleshoot remotes, keypads, and wall controls, including battery, programming, and compatibility issues. Whether a particular remote can be repaired or needs replacing depends on its age and model, and we cannot promise every remote or opener can be repaired.

Do you repair chain-drive openers?

Generally yes, depending on the model and parts availability. Chain-drive problems include a loose or noisy chain, a worn trolley, or a failed motor or gear. Call with your opener's brand and model so we can tell you whether we can service it.

Do you service belt-drive openers?

Generally yes, again subject to model and parts. Belt drives are quieter but can have worn or slipping belts, trolley issues, and the same motor and board faults as other openers. Tell us the brand and model when you call.

Can you replace a garage door opener?

Yes, when diagnosis shows replacement makes sense. Replacement depends on door weight, configuration, and compatibility, and we explain the options before work begins. Replacement is not the first answer. Many opener problems are simpler. See our opener replacement page.

Can you repair safety sensors?

Yes. We check sensor alignment, lenses, wiring, and function, and test the automatic reversal afterward. In Boston winters, snow, ice, and salt spray can affect the sensors near the floor. We never bypass or disable them.

Can cold Boston weather stop an opener from working?

It can contribute. Cold stiffens lubricants and rubber seals, ice can freeze a door to the floor, and snow can block sensors. An opener forced against a frozen door can strain or fail. If the door will not move, do not keep pressing the button. Clear what you safely can and call.

My opener runs but the door doesn't move. Is the opener broken?

Not necessarily. The carriage may be disconnected from the door, a drive gear or belt may have failed, or a broken spring may be keeping the door too heavy to lift. Do not try to reconnect or lift an unstable door. A technician should check both the opener and the door.

Is it worth repairing a garage door opener?

Often, yes, if the cause is a control, sensor, or modest drive fault and the model is supported. Replacement makes more sense when the motor or board has failed beyond sensible repair, parts are unavailable, or the model is obsolete. We explain both options and do not push replacement.

Do shared or condo garages affect opener repair?

They can. Shared or multi-unit garages may involve other residents' remotes, signal interference, building rules, or limited access to the opener. Tell us if the garage is shared, who manages it, and whether access or permission is needed when you call.
