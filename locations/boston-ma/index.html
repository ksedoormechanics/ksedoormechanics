Common Garage Door Problems in Boston

New England winters, road salt and damp weather can wear doors and hardware. These are the problems we hear about most.

Door will not open or close

Causes range from power and remote batteries to sensors, springs and cables. See door not opening.

Broken or worn springs

A loud bang, a visible gap or a door that feels heavy points to springs. Leave this to a professional. See spring replacement.

Damaged cables

Frayed, loose or off-drum cables can leave a door crooked. See cable repair.

Noisy garage doors

Worn rollers, dry hinges or a door out of balance. See noisy door repair.

Opener problems

Motors, remotes, keypads, wall controls and sensors. See opener repair.

Misaligned or bent tracks

Tracks that are bent or out of line can jam the door. See track repair.

Worn rollers

Rollers wear and can bind or grind. See roller replacement.

Routine maintenance

Regular inspections catch wear early. See maintenance.

Safety first

Springs, cables and bottom brackets are under extreme tension. Do not adjust or remove them yourself. Safe first checks are the remote battery, power to the opener and wiping the sensor lenses. If the door is crooked, a spring has a gap or a cable looks frayed, stop using it and call.

Getting a Fair Garage Door Repair Quote

Homeowners often worry about being overcharged. A good repair visit should be easy to follow. Ask:

  • What is wrong with the door or opener?
  • Which parts need replacing, and why?
  • Is repair or replacement the better choice?
  • What will be tested when the job is finished?
  • Is anything else needed for the door to work safely?

We do not publish prices, because they depend on the door and the repair. We inspect first and explain the options before you approve work.

Renting or in a Condo?

If you rent, check your lease or ask your landlord before you approve repairs. In a condo or association property, check who owns the door and whether building approval is needed. This is general information, not legal advice. Tell us about the building when you call.

My garage door will not open. What can I safely check first?

Check that the opener has power, replace the remote battery and wipe the sensor lenses near the floor. If you see a gap in a spring, a loose or frayed cable, or the door is crooked or off track, stop and call. Do not try to adjust springs or cables yourself.
