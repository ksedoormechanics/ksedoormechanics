Garage Door Spring Replacement in East Boston

Garage door spring replacement is the professional removal of a worn or broken counterbalance spring and installation of a correctly matched replacement. Springs carry the door's weight, so a failed spring can leave the door too heavy to lift. KSE Door Mechanics inspects the system and explains the work before it starts.

Symptoms That May Indicate a Spring Problem

Any of these may point to a spring issue, but each can have other causes:

  • A loud bang or snap from the garage
  • Door feels unusually heavy or will not open normally
  • Door opens only partly or moves unevenly
  • A visible gap or separation in a spring
  • Slack cables at the bottom corners
  • Opener strains, hums or stops
  • Inconsistent movement

Safety first

Springs are high-tension parts. Stop using a door that looks damaged or unstable, keep people and pets away, and do not touch springs, cables, drums or brackets. Call a qualified professional.

Spring Replacement FAQs

What are common signs of a broken garage door spring?

Common signs include a loud bang from the garage, a visible gap in a spring coil, a door that feels very heavy or won't lift, uneven movement, slack cables, or an opener that strains and stops. These symptoms can have other causes, so a professional should inspect the door.

Can I operate my garage door if a spring appears broken?

No. Stop using the door, keep people and pets clear, and call a professional. Forcing a door with a failed spring can damage the door and opener and can cause injury.

Is garage door spring replacement a DIY job?

No. Garage door springs hold a lot of stored tension and can cause serious injury. KSE Door Mechanics does not provide DIY winding or replacement instructions; have a qualified technician handle it.

What is the difference between torsion and extension springs?

Torsion springs are mounted on a shaft above the door and twist to counterbalance it. Extension springs run along the horizontal tracks and stretch. The right type depends on your door's design and weight.

Should both springs be replaced at once?

On a two-spring door, technicians often recommend replacing both because they have worked through the same cycles. Whether that fits your door is decided at inspection.

What causes a garage door spring to fail?

Springs are rated for a limited number of open-and-close cycles, so wear is the most common cause. Corrosion, an unbalanced door and poor maintenance can shorten life. Cold weather is often blamed, but cycle wear is the underlying factor.

How long does a garage door spring last?

Springs are rated in cycles, where one open and one close equals one cycle. Real lifespan depends on the spring's rating, how often you use the door and its balance, so there is no single number.

Does spring replacement require inspecting other components?

Usually a technician looks at cables, drums, rollers, tracks and brackets too, because a failed spring can leave them stressed. Extra work is recommended only if the inspection shows a need, and it needs your approval.

What affects the cost of garage door spring replacement?

Spring type, size and cycle rating, door weight, one spring versus a pair, related hardware condition and the scope of work all affect cost. Ask for the scope in writing before approving work. No prices are advertised here.

Is it worth fixing a garage door spring?

Replacing a failed spring is usually the practical repair on a sound door. If the door is also badly damaged, replacement of the door may be worth comparing.
