Signs a Garage Door Spring May Be Damaged

Suddenly hard to lift

Or it will not lift at all.

Uneven opening

One side rises ahead of the other.

Feels very heavy

The door no longer feels balanced.

Stops partway

It will not stay where you leave it.

Visible gap or break

A spring looks separated or broken.

Loud bang

Then the door stops working normally.

Opener struggles

It strains, hums or stops.

Door will not hold position

It drifts or drops from halfway.

These signs can have other causes, such as cables, rollers, tracks or the opener, so professional assessment is needed to confirm.

Torsion vs Extension Springs

Torsion springs mount on a shaft above the door and twist to counterbalance its weight. Extension springs run along the side tracks and stretch. The right spring depends on the door's size, weight, configuration and hardware, so matching it correctly matters.

A wrong spring can leave the door too heavy, make it rise too quickly, stop it closing correctly or strain the opener. More detail: torsion spring replacement and extension spring replacement.

What are common signs of a broken garage door spring?

A loud bang, a door that feels unusually heavy or will not lift, a door that rises unevenly or stops partway, an opener that strains, or a visible gap or separation in the spring. These can have other causes, so a technician should confirm.

Is it safe to use a garage door with a broken spring?

No. Without working springs the door can be extremely heavy and may drop or drag. Stop using it, keep people and pets clear, and call for service. Do not try to lift it by hand or push the opener to force it.

Can I manually lift my garage door if the spring is broken?

We advise against it. A door without spring support can weigh far more than it feels safe to lift and can fall. Leave it closed if it is, keep clear of it, and request professional service.

Can I replace a garage door spring myself?

We do not recommend it. Springs hold significant stored energy and can cause serious injury if handled wrongly. This page gives no replacement steps on purpose. Please book a qualified technician.

Should both garage door springs be replaced together?

On a two-spring door, many technicians recommend replacing both, because the second spring has typically seen the same wear. Your technician will advise after inspecting your system.

How long do garage door springs last?

It depends on the spring's cycle rating, how often the door is used, the door's weight and its condition. Springs are commonly rated in cycles, and one open and close counts as one cycle. Heavy daily use wears them sooner.

What is the difference between torsion and extension springs?

Torsion springs mount on a shaft above the door and twist to counterbalance it. Extension springs run along the horizontal tracks and stretch. Which one your door needs depends on its size, weight, configuration and hardware.

Should garage door cables be inspected when a spring is replaced?

Yes, it is sensible. Cables, drums, rollers, tracks and brackets work with the spring, and a broken spring can stress them. We check related hardware and door balance as part of the service.

What does a spring replacement quote usually include?

Ask that it names the spring type and size, whether one or both springs are replaced, any cable, drum or hardware work, balance testing, and the final safety check. A clear, itemized scope helps you compare quotes.

Why does the right spring size matter?

The spring must match the door's weight and height. A wrong spring can make the door feel too heavy, rise too fast, fail to close properly, or strain the opener and hardware.
