Common brand-related garage door problems

Opener not responding

Check the power outlet, wall control and remote battery first. If the opener still does nothing, the logic board or power supply may need testing.

Remote not connecting

Weak batteries or a lost pairing are common. Remotes and keypads must match the opener's frequency and code type.

Door not closing

Usually a sensor or limit issue. A door that reverses on its way down is acting on a safety signal worth checking.

Safety sensors not aligned

Sensors face each other near the floor. A bumped bracket or a dirty lens can break the beam and stop the door.

Unusual opener noise

Grinding, rattling or squealing can come from worn drive parts, dry rollers or an unbalanced door.

Motor runs, door doesn't move

Often a broken spring or a stripped drive gear. If the door feels very heavy by hand, stop and call us.

Broken spring

A loud bang followed by a door that won't lift often means a broken spring. Do not force the door.

Door moving unevenly

One side higher than the other points to a cable, track or spring problem that needs professional attention.

Worn cables or rollers

Frayed cables and chipped rollers make doors noisy and unsafe. Cables are under tension and should not be handled yourself.

Replacement compatibility

Replacement parts and openers must suit your door's weight, size and brand. Send us the label details and we will check.

When to call a professional

Springs, cables and bottom brackets are under extreme tension, and opener electronics need proper testing. If the door is stuck, uneven or very heavy by hand, stop using it and call us.

Get Help With Your Garage Door

How do I identify my garage door brand?

Check for a manufacturer label on the inside face of the door, usually on one of the panels or along an edge. For the opener, check the label on the motor housing mounted to the ceiling. You never need to take anything apart to find either one.

On the door

Look for a label or stamped name on the inside face of a section, near an edge or at the bottom. It often shows the manufacturer, model and date.

On the opener

The motor housing carries a label with the brand, model and serial number. Read it from the floor or a stable stepladder. Do not remove covers.

Remotes & wall controls

The brand name is usually printed on the front or back of the remote and the wall control button.

Paperwork

Original installation documents, receipts, manuals and warranty cards usually name the manufacturer and model.

Safety & compliance labels

Warning and compliance stickers on the door, springs and opener often include the maker's name.

The door and opener may differ

The door and opener are often from different companies. Identify each separately, and send us photos of both if you are unsure.

Can you repair a LiftMaster garage door opener?

Yes. We diagnose LiftMaster motors, logic boards, safety sensors, remotes, keypads and myQ connection problems. If the unit is old or parts are hard to find, we will explain replacement options. See LiftMaster opener repair.

Do you work on Chamberlain garage door openers?

Yes. We repair and replace Chamberlain chain, belt and Wi-Fi openers, and we program remotes and keypads. See Chamberlain opener repair.

Can a Genie opener be repaired?

In many cases, yes. Common Genie repairs include sensors, remotes and Intellicode re-pairing, travel and force settings, and worn drive parts. When the opener is beyond economical repair, we go over replacement. See Genie opener repair.

Do you service Craftsman garage door openers?

Yes. We repair Craftsman openers and program their remotes and keypads. Because many are older units, we will tell you honestly whether repair or replacement makes more sense. See Craftsman opener repair.

Can you replace a Clopay garage door panel?

Often, yes, when a matching Clopay section is available for your door model and color. Older or discontinued designs may be harder to match, in which case we discuss alternatives. See Clopay panel replacement.

Do you repair Wayne Dalton spring systems?

Yes. We service Wayne Dalton doors, including TorqueMaster spring systems. These springs are under high tension and should never be adjusted without training, so please call us rather than attempting it. See Wayne Dalton TorqueMaster spring repair.

Does my garage door have to match my opener brand?

No. The door and the opener are often made by different companies. What matters is that the opener suits the door's weight, size and type, and that the safety sensors and force settings are set correctly. Our opener installation service pairs openers with your existing door.

How do I find my garage door manufacturer?

Look for a label on the inside face of the door, usually on one of the sections or along an edge. Check the paperwork from the original installation too. For the opener, read the label on the motor housing. See how to identify your brand below. You do not need to take anything apart.

Can you replace an older discontinued garage door opener?

Yes. When an opener can no longer be repaired or its parts are discontinued, we can install a current opener and reconnect your remotes and keypads. See opener replacement.

Do you install smart garage door openers?

Yes. We install openers with built-in Wi-Fi and help with app setup. A working home Wi-Fi signal in the garage and a compatible phone are needed. See smart opener installation and Wi-Fi opener setup.
