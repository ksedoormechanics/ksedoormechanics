Why Won't My Garage Door Open?

A garage door usually won't open because of one of a few things: the opener has no power, the remote or wall control isn't sending a signal, a spring or cable has failed, the opener's drive has failed, or a roller or track is binding. The right diagnosis depends on whether the opener responds and whether the door tries to move. A door with a broken spring or other mechanical fault should not be forced open.

Why Won't My Garage Door Open With the Remote?

A remote that does nothing does not mean the door is broken. A weak battery, lost programming, a nearby signal that interferes, or a receiver or power problem can all stop it. Outside keypads and wall-mounted remotes have batteries too. Some openers have a lock or vacation mode that disables remotes but not the wall button.

Safe checks: replace the remote battery, try the wall button, and read your opener's manual for programming and lock features. If neither the remote nor the wall button works, look at power next.

Why Is My Garage Door Wall Button Not Working?

Common causes are no power to the opener, a defective wall control, a wiring fault, or a control board problem. If the wall button is dead but the motor responds to the remote, the wall control is the likely culprit. If the opener clicks or hums, the problem is more likely mechanical.

Some models have a lock mode with different indicators. Check your manual. Do not open the opener housing or touch exposed wiring; electrical faults need a qualified professional.

Why Does My Garage Door Opener Run but the Door Doesn't Move?

If the motor runs but the door stays put, the link between opener and door may be broken. Possible causes include a disconnected carriage, a worn drive gear, a broken belt or chain, or a failed trolley. A noisy motor does not prove the opener needs replacing. A heavy door can also leave a healthy motor running with no result.

Do not try to reconnect or release a door that may be unstable. See opener services.

Why Does My Garage Door Open a Few Inches Then Stop?

This usually means the opener is meeting abnormal resistance and protecting itself. Causes include a broken spring, poor counterbalance, a binding roller or track, a cable problem, or travel and force settings. Some doors rise slightly and drop again, or work only now and then.

Do not increase the opener's force to push through. A poorly balanced door needs a mechanical fix. See balance adjustment.

Can a Broken Garage Door Spring Stop the Door From Opening?

Yes, and it is one of the most common reasons. Torsion springs above the door and extension springs along its sides offset the door's weight. When one breaks, the door can be extremely heavy and unsafe to raise. Signs include a loud bang, a visible gap in the coil, a hum with no lift, and a door that will not stay up. Fatigue usually builds over time. After any spring work, correct, matched springs matter.

Do not lift the door, wind, or adjust springs. See spring replacement, torsion springs, and extension springs.

Can Garage Door Cables Prevent the Door From Opening?

Yes. Lift cables and drums lift both sides together. A frayed or broken cable, or one that has slipped off its drum, can leave the door crooked, uneven, or stuck, and potentially unstable. A crooked door may have unequal cable movement rather than an opener fault.

Cables are under tension. Do not reset or tension them. See cable repair and drum replacement.

Can Worn Rollers or Tracks Keep a Garage Door From Opening?

Worn nylon or steel rollers, failed roller bearings, a binding or misaligned track, displaced rollers, damaged hinges, or loose support brackets can all add friction. A mechanical obstruction stops the door or makes the opener strain.

Do not force rollers into tracks or realign a compromised door. See roller replacement, track repair, and off-track repair.

What If My Garage Door Opener Has No Power?

A silent opener often has lost power, and in power-outage questions it is the leading answer. Safe, low-risk observations include:

  • Do the opener's lights or controls respond?
  • Has a relevant breaker tripped?
  • Is an accessible plug disconnected?
  • Could the remote or wall control be the fault?
  • Does the opener show a model-specific status light or beeping?

Do not open the housing or touch wiring. If you see smoke, smell burning, or notice sparking, stay clear and contact a qualified professional or emergency service.

Why Is My Garage Door Opener Humming but Not Lifting?

Humming means the motor is powered but cannot move the load. The cause can be the motor, gears, trolley, chain, belt, or screw drive, a control board fault, a mechanical load, or a door counterbalance failure. A broken spring makes the door heavy; a worn gear leaves the door light but the motor spinning.

Do not use the opener to force a mechanically unstable door to move.

Can Garage Door Safety Sensors Stop a Door From Opening?

On most residential openers, photo-eye sensors prevent unsafe automatic closing. A blocked or misaligned sensor typically shows up when you try to close the door, not open it. Model behavior and indicator lights vary, and beeping or flashing can also point to an electrical fault, so check your manual. Opening and closing problems are different problems.

Never disconnect or bypass sensors. See opener services and sensor repair.

What Can You Safely Check When Your Garage Door Won't Open?

Only non-invasive checks, from a safe place.

  • See whether the opener has power.
  • Check the remote battery.
  • Review wall-control settings, including any lock mode.
  • Note whether the opener makes noise when activated.
  • Look for obvious obstructions from a safe distance.
  • Look for a visibly broken spring or cable without approaching or touching it.
  • Note if the door looks crooked or unstable.
  • Read the opener manufacturer's manual.
  • Stop using the door if a mechanical problem is suspected.

Do not force the door, pull an emergency release while the door may be unstable, lift a door with a broken spring, adjust springs or cables, push rollers into tracks, raise opener force, or bypass safety equipment.

When Is a Garage Door That Won't Open Unsafe?

Stop operating the door when:

  • A spring appears broken or you heard a loud bang.
  • The door is unusually heavy.
  • A lift cable is frayed or displaced.
  • Rollers have come off the track.
  • The door hangs crooked or its sections look unstable.
  • The opener strains against resistance.
  • Movement is unpredictable.
  • There is smoke or a burning smell from the opener.

Keep people and pets clear. Do not force the opener or lift an unstable door. For an urgent problem, see emergency garage door repair and call to check availability.

Garage Door Not Opening FAQs

Why won't my garage door open?

The most common causes are no power to the opener, a remote or wall control problem, a broken spring or cable, a failed opener drive, or a binding roller or track. What you hear and whether the door tries to move helps narrow it down. If the door feels heavy or looks crooked, stop and call a professional.

Why is my garage door not opening with the remote?

The remote battery may be weak, the remote may have lost its programming, or the opener's receiver or power may be at fault. Try the wall button. If it works, the problem is likely the remote. If neither works, check power. Also confirm the wall control's lock feature is off.

Why doesn't the wall button open my garage door?

The opener may have no power, the wall control may be defective or have a wiring fault, or the control board may have a problem. A lock mode can disable remotes but usually not the wall button. Do not open the opener housing or touch wiring. Call us if the button stays dead.

Why does my garage door opener hum but not work?

Humming usually means the motor is getting power but cannot move the load. The cause may be a worn drive gear, a drive or trolley problem, or a door that is too heavy because a spring has failed. Do not keep pressing the button. Have a technician inspect the door and opener.

Why does the garage door motor run without lifting the door?

The opener may be disconnected from the door, or its drive gears, belt, chain, or trolley may have failed. If the carriage was released, the motor can run while the door stays put. Do not try to reattach an unstable door yourself. A technician can find the cause.

Why does my garage door open a few inches and stop?

This usually means the opener meets abnormal resistance. Possible causes include a broken spring, poor balance, a binding roller or track, a cable problem, or the opener's travel and force settings. Do not raise the opener's force to push through. The door needs a mechanical inspection.

Can a broken spring prevent a garage door from opening?

Yes. Springs offset the door's weight, so without one the door can be extremely heavy and the opener may only hum or lift a few inches. A loud bang or a gap in the spring coil are signs. Do not lift the door or touch the spring; call a professional.

Can a broken garage door cable stop the door from opening?

Yes. Lift cables carry the door's weight on both sides. A broken or displaced cable can make the door crooked, uneven, or stuck. Cables are under tension, so keep clear and do not try to reset them. A technician should inspect the cable and drum.

Can worn rollers prevent a garage door from moving?

They can. Worn or seized rollers bind in the track, which adds resistance and can stop the door partway. Worn rollers often cause noise and rough movement first. Do not push rollers back into a track yourself. Roller replacement is a standard professional repair.

Can a misaligned track stop a garage door from opening?

Yes. A track that is out of position or bent can bind the rollers and jam the door. The door may hang crooked or grind as it moves. Do not force it. A technician can check the track, rollers, and hardware and decide on repair or replacement.

Why is my garage door unusually heavy?

A heavy door usually means the springs are no longer carrying its weight, often because one has broken or worn out. Cables or binding hardware can also add resistance. Do not lift the door or run the opener against it. Keep clear and have it inspected.

Can a power outage affect the garage door opener?

Yes. An opener with no power will not respond to the remote or wall control. Check whether other outlets or lights work and whether a breaker has tripped. If power is fine and the opener is still dead, or if you see smoke or smell burning, stay clear and call a qualified professional.

Can garage door sensors prevent the door from opening?

Usually not. On most residential openers the photo-eye sensors stop the door from closing on an obstruction, so sensor problems mostly show up when closing. Behavior varies by model, and indicator lights or beeping may signal a fault. Never bypass the sensors. Check your manual or call us.

What is the difference between an opener problem and a mechanical door problem?

An opener problem involves power, controls, the motor, or the drive. A mechanical problem involves the door's springs, cables, rollers, tracks, or hinges. If the motor runs but the door is heavy, binds, or hangs crooked, the cause is often mechanical. A technician usually checks both.

Is it safe to manually lift a garage door that won't open?

Not if you suspect a broken spring, cable problem, or off-track door. The door can be very heavy and may fall or twist. Keep people and pets clear and do not stand under it. Let a qualified professional assess it before it is moved.

Does a garage door that won't open need a new opener?

Not necessarily. Many cases involve a dead battery, lost power, a controls issue, or a broken spring rather than a failed opener. A new opener is considered when the motor, drive, or board has failed beyond sensible repair or the model is obsolete. An inspection shows which.

When should I stop trying to open my garage door?

Stop if you hear a loud bang, see a gap in a spring, find a frayed or displaced cable, see rollers out of the track, notice the door hanging crooked or very heavy, or smell smoke or burning from the opener. Keep clear and call a professional.

What affects the cost of repairing a garage door that won't open?

The root cause matters most: opener or mechanical failure, which parts are involved, spring or cable work, roller and track condition, door size and weight, and whether it is residential or commercial. We do not list prices here, and some causes are simple. An inspection defines the work.
