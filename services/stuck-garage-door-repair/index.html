Why Is My Garage Door Stuck?

A garage door gets stuck when something stops it moving: a broken spring, a cable fault, a binding roller, a bent or blocked track, an opener or power failure, or a safety sensor interruption. The cause depends on whether the door is stuck open, closed or halfway. If the door is crooked, partly open or looks unsupported, do not force it. Call a professional.

Different Ways a Garage Door Can Become Stuck

Start with where the door stopped. Each condition points to a different set of causes.

Stuck open

Looks like: the door won't close, or starts down and reverses.

Possible causes: opener controls, blocked photo eyes, obstructions, travel settings, track or roller binding, cable or spring faults.

Safe observation: check from a safe spot whether the sensor lights are lit.

Stop if: the door hangs crooked or moves unpredictably.

Stuck closed

Looks like: the door won't rise, or the opener hums or strains.

Possible causes: broken spring, failed cable, power loss, disengaged opener drive, mechanical binding.

Safe observation: note any loud bang you heard and any gap visible in the spring.

Stop if: the door feels heavy. Never lift it by hand.

Stuck halfway

Looks like: the door stops partway up or down, or hangs uneven.

Possible causes: roller or track binding, cable drum faults, spring or balance problems, opener stopping on resistance.

Safe observation: keep the area clear and look for a crooked edge.

Stop if: always. A partly open door may be unstable.

Why Is My Garage Door Stuck Open?

Most often the door cannot close because something blocks its path or trips a safety feature: an obstruction, a blocked or misaligned photo eye, opener travel settings, binding rollers or tracks, or a spring or cable fault.

Safety features should never be bypassed to close a malfunctioning door, and you should not push the door downward by hand. If the opener cycles but the door keeps reversing, the fault may be a sensor, or it may be mechanical resistance that the opener is detecting.

Why Won't My Garage Door Open?

A door that will not open is often held down by a broken counterbalance spring, a failed lift cable, a power loss, an opener drive problem or mechanical binding.

A door with a failed spring can be unexpectedly heavy, and the opener is not designed to make up for it. Do not forcibly lift the door. Listen for whether the motor runs, and note whether the door moved at all.

Why Is My Garage Door Stuck Halfway?

A door stopped partway usually points to roller binding, track alignment, a cable drum fault, counterbalance failure, mechanical resistance or the opener cutting out on load.

A partially open door may be unstable. Keep people, pets and vehicles clear, and call for inspection rather than trying to move it. We do not provide manual release or lifting steps for a door in this condition.

What Can You Safely Check When Your Garage Door Won't Move?

These are observations only. Do them from a safe distance and stop if anything looks wrong.

  • Check from a safe spot whether the opener light or display has power.
  • Look for objects in the door's path without approaching an unstable door.
  • See whether the photo-eye lights appear blocked or misaligned. Do not bypass them.
  • Consider whether the remote battery is depleted.
  • Check whether a wall control shows a lock or vacation mode.
  • Notice whether the door hangs crooked or uneven.
  • Note unusual noises without repeatedly operating the door.
  • In winter, notice whether the bottom seal looks frozen to the floor. Don't pry or force it.

Do not

  • Force a stuck door or lift a possibly unbalanced one.
  • Release the opener if the door is unstable or crooked.
  • Remove brackets or adjust spring tension.
  • Rewind cables or force rollers into tracks.
  • Bypass safety sensors or raise opener force.

Can a Broken Garage Door Spring Cause the Door to Get Stuck?

Yes. Torsion or extension springs counterbalance the door's weight, and when one fails the door can stay closed, lift unevenly or stop partway.

The lift cables and drums work with the springs, so a spring fault can show up as a cable problem and the reverse. The opener should not be used to overcome a failed counterbalance system. We do not describe winding or tension-release steps because they are hazardous.

Can Worn Rollers or Tracks Cause a Garage Door to Stick?

Yes. Worn roller bearings, bent or loose tracks, and misaligned brackets create friction, so the door can bind and stop.

A roller problem tends to cause grinding or jerky movement, while a track problem often stops the door at the same point each time, such as the curved transition. We do not provide roller-resetting or track adjustment steps.

Can a Garage Door Get Stuck Because of a Cable Problem?

Yes. A frayed, slack or displaced lift cable, or a cable off its drum, can make the door lift unevenly, hang crooked or jam.

Cables carry the door's load under high tension, so uneven or crooked movement means the door may be unstable. Do not tension cables or adjust drums.

Why Does My Garage Door Opener Run but the Door Won't Move?

The motor may be running while the drive, trolley or release is not engaging the door, or while a spring or cable fault keeps the door from lifting.

Chain and belt drives, trolleys, travel settings, power, wall controls and remotes can all be involved, and some brands have lock modes. Motor noise does not prove the opener is the faulty part.

Can Garage Door Safety Sensors Prevent the Door From Closing?

Yes. If the photo-eye beam is blocked, dirty, misaligned or has lost power, the door may refuse to close or may reverse.

Sensors commonly affect closing but are not behind every stuck door. Never disconnect or bypass them, defeat the reversal system, or force the door closed repeatedly.

Why Do Garage Doors Get Stuck More Often in Boston Winters?

Cold, moisture and freeze-thaw cycles can make a door stick: a bottom seal may freeze to the floor, corrosion can stiffen hardware, and older systems carry more wear. Springs, cables and openers all work harder in the cold.

If the seal looks frozen, don't pry or force the door, and don't run the opener against it. A power outage can also leave a door stopped in place. We don't publish local failure statistics. For maintenance, see garage door maintenance.

When Should You Stop Operating a Stuck Garage Door?

Stop if the door is crooked or partly unsupported, a spring looks broken, a cable is displaced or frayed, a roller is outside its track, the door binds or moves unpredictably, the opener strains, or safety systems malfunction.

Don't stand beneath an unstable door. Keep children and pets away. Don't force the opener or lift a heavy door, and don't tamper with springs, drums or loaded brackets.

Stuck Garage Door FAQs

Why is my garage door stuck?

The usual causes are a broken spring, a cable or roller problem, a bent or blocked track, an opener or power fault, or a safety sensor interruption. Where it stopped (open, closed or partway) and what you heard help narrow it down. A technician needs to inspect the door to confirm the cause.

Why is my garage door stuck closed?

Common causes include a broken counterbalance spring, a failed lift cable, a power loss, a disengaged or failed opener drive, or mechanical binding. A door with a failed spring can be very heavy, so do not try to lift it.

Why is my garage door stuck open?

Often the door cannot close because of an obstruction, a blocked or misaligned photo-eye sensor, opener travel settings, or binding in the tracks or rollers. Never bypass the sensors to force the door down.

Why is my garage door stuck halfway?

Typical causes are roller or track binding, uneven cables, a weak or broken spring, or the opener stopping on resistance. A door stopped partway may be unstable, so keep people and vehicles clear and avoid operating it.

What causes a garage door to stop moving?

Power loss, a tripped safety system, opener drive failure, broken springs, damaged cables, rollers out of the track, or a bent track. Several of these look alike, which is why inspection matters.

Can a broken spring cause a garage door to stay closed?

Yes. Springs carry most of the door's weight. When one breaks, the opener often cannot lift the door, or it strains and stops. Do not force the door. Spring replacement should be done by a professional.

Can a broken cable cause a garage door to get stuck?

Yes. A frayed, slack or displaced lift cable can make the door lift unevenly, hang crooked or jam. Do not try to re-seat or tighten cables, because they are under high tension.

Can worn rollers make a garage door stick?

Worn or damaged rollers can bind in the track, causing jerky movement, grinding or stopping. Because rollers sit in the track beside loaded hardware, replacement is best left to a technician.

Can a misaligned track prevent a garage door from opening?

Yes. A bent, loose or misaligned track can pinch rollers and stop the door partway. Operating the opener against a binding track can cause further damage.

Why does my opener run but the garage door doesn't move?

The motor may be running while the drive, trolley or release is disengaged, or while a broken spring or cable keeps the door from lifting. Motor noise alone does not show which part has failed.

Can safety sensors prevent a garage door from closing?

Yes. If the photo-eye beam is blocked, dirty, misaligned or has lost power, the door may refuse to close or reverse. Sensors do not explain every stuck door, and they should never be bypassed.

Can a power outage leave a garage door stuck?

Yes. A door can stay in place when power fails, and a trip or reset at the panel may be involved. Check only from a safe position, and don't force a door that looks crooked or unsupported.

Is it safe to operate a garage door that is stuck halfway?

No. Repeated operation can worsen binding, strain the opener or drop the door. Keep people, pets and vehicles away and contact a professional.

Should I manually lift a stuck garage door?

No. If the spring or cable has failed, the door may be far heavier than it looks and can fall. Do not lift it, pull it into place or stand beneath it.

Is an off-track garage door the same as a stuck garage door?

Not exactly. Off-track means a roller or cable has left its path, so the door often looks crooked. A stuck door may be fully in the track but blocked by a spring, opener, sensor or binding fault.

Can an unbalanced garage door become stuck?

Yes. Poor balance makes the door heavy, uneven or likely to slam, which strains the opener and can stop travel. Balance problems often trace back to springs, so a technician should evaluate them.

Does a stuck garage door always need a new opener?

No. Many stuck doors come from springs, cables, rollers, tracks or sensors rather than the opener. A diagnosis should identify the failing part before replacement is considered.
