Quick answer: A heavy door, a door that won't open or lifts only partway, uneven movement, a loud snap, a visible gap in the spring and a straining opener are the main signs. Some can come from cables, tracks or the opener, so a professional should find the cause.

Please don't force the door open to find out what's wrong. A door that has lost its counterbalance can be heavy and can fall.

How Garage Door Springs Support Safe Door Movement

Quick answer: Springs counterbalance the weight of the door so it can be lifted and lowered under control. The opener provides powered movement, but it is not designed to carry the door's weight.

The counterbalance system includes torsion or extension springs, lift cables, cable drums, pulleys, shafts, bearings and mounting hardware. They work as one system, so a fault in any part can affect the others. We explain the parts, not adjustment steps, because those are high-risk.

What each part does

  • Springs: store and release energy to offset door weight
  • Lift cables: transfer that force to the door
  • Cable drums and pulleys: guide and wind the cables
  • Shaft and bearings: support torsion springs
  • Opener: moves the door, with springs carrying the load

Torsion Spring Repair and Inspection

  • Mounted on a torsion shaft above the door
  • Single or dual spring arrangements
  • Work with cable drums and bearings
  • Failure signs: gap in the coil, heavy door
  • Worn shaft or bearings can add noise and binding
  • A broken spring is replaced, never rejoined or welded

How Cables and Drums Affect Spring System Performance

Lift cables connect the springs to the door, and cable drums wind and release them on torsion systems. A worn cable, slipped cable or damaged drum can make the door tilt, bind or feel heavy, so it can look like a spring failure. One cable tight and the other loose is a common clue.

A cable problem doesn't always mean new springs, and a spring problem doesn't always mean new cables. We evaluate them together. Cable repair

Why Spring and Hardware Compatibility Matters

Quick answer: Springs and related hardware must match the door's type, weight, size and counterbalance setup. Similar-looking parts may not be interchangeable, and a mismatch can leave the door too heavy, too light or unbalanced.

Professionals consider spring type and specification, door weight and dimensions, shaft and drum configuration, cable compatibility, pulley arrangement, manufacturer guidance and operating cycles. We don't publish winding formulas or adjustment steps.

Problems after a spring job?

Homeowners report doors that shake, grind or won't close after spring work. Causes can include mismatched springs, loose spring anchors, a bowed shaft or unevenly set cables. A proper job checks each of these before handover. If your door misbehaves after service, call the installer or us to inspect it.

Garage Door Spring Repair FAQs

What does garage door spring repair include?

It usually means diagnosing the spring and counterbalance system: inspecting springs, cables, drums, pulleys and bearings, assessing balance, repairing related hardware and replacing failed springs when needed, then testing operation.

Can a broken garage door spring be repaired?

A fully broken spring is replaced, not repaired. We don't weld, splice or reconnect springs. Some problems that look like spring failure are cable, drum or balance issues, which can be repaired after diagnosis.

How do I know if my garage door spring is broken?

Look for a loud bang, a gap in a torsion spring, a door that's very heavy or opens only a few inches, uneven movement, loose cables or a straining opener. Other faults can look similar, so have it confirmed.

Is it safe to open a garage door with a broken spring?

We advise against it. The door may be very heavy and can drop or tilt, and forcing it can damage the opener, cables and tracks. Keep clear and call for service.

What is the difference between torsion and extension springs?

Torsion springs mount on a shaft above the door and twist to counterbalance it. Extension springs run along the tracks and stretch. Their hardware and failure signs differ.

Why is my garage door suddenly heavy?

The springs may have broken or lost tension, so the opener or your hands are lifting the full weight. Cable, drum or track problems can also add resistance. Don't keep forcing it.

Can a spring problem affect my garage door opener?

Yes. An opener isn't meant to lift an unbalanced door, so it can strain, hum, reverse or wear faster. Fixing the counterbalance problem protects the opener.

What causes garage door springs to fail?

Repeated cycles, metal fatigue, corrosion, poor balance, incorrect spring selection and worn related hardware are common causes. Use and maintenance change how long springs last.

Can a garage door cable problem look like a spring failure?

Yes. A frayed, slipped or uneven cable can cause crooked or heavy movement similar to a spring fault. That's why we inspect springs, cables, drums and pulleys together.

Should both garage door springs be replaced?

Often it's worth considering, since both have had the same use, but it isn't mandatory in every case. We check age, condition, specifications and balance before advising.

What is included in a professional spring inspection?

We check spring type and condition, door size and weight needs, cables, drums, pulleys, shafts, bearings, brackets, track and roller movement, balance and opener operation.

How is garage door balance checked?

Technicians assess how the door travels and holds its position using appropriate methods for the system. Some online videos suggest testing it yourself, but a weak or broken spring can make the door drop, so we don't advise it.

How long do garage door springs last?

It varies with the spring's cycle rating, use, corrosion and maintenance. Standard springs are commonly rated for a limited number of cycles, and higher-cycle options exist. We don't promise a fixed lifespan.

What affects garage door spring repair cost?

Cost depends on the real cause, spring configuration, door size and weight, number of springs, cable, drum or pulley condition, compatibility and service scope. Call for service information, and ask what's included.
