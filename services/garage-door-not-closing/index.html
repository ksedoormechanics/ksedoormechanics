Why Won't My Garage Door Close?

A garage door won't close when a safety or control system blocks it, or when something mechanical resists it. Common causes are a blocked, dirty or misaligned photo-eye sensor, opener control or power problems, close-travel settings, and roller or track binding. The pattern matters: no response, reversing, or stopping above the floor each point to different faults. Don't force the door closed or bypass its sensors. Call a professional.

Can Garage Door Safety Sensors Prevent the Door From Closing?

Yes. Photo-eye sensors send a beam across the opening, and if it is interrupted or the system malfunctions, the opener may refuse to close or may reverse.

A beam can be broken by an object, dirt on a lens, a bumped bracket, a wiring fault or lost power. Indicator lights vary by manufacturer, so use your opener's manual to read them. Sunlight shining into a lens can also confuse a sensor, which is one reason a door may fail only at certain times of day. Never bypass or disable the sensors.

Why Does My Garage Door Start Closing Then Go Back Up?

Most often the opener is reversing on purpose, because a sensor beam was interrupted, something is in the path, or the door is meeting resistance.

Reversal exists to prevent entrapment. Causes include photo-eye interruption or misalignment, an object in the travel path, binding rollers or tracks, and opener force or travel behavior. Don't try to defeat the reversal or increase force; a technician finds what is triggering it.

Why Does My Garage Door Stop Before Reaching the Floor?

The opener may be set to stop early, something may block the door, or resistance may be tripping the opener.

A door that stops above the floor is different from one that touches the floor and immediately reverses. Possible causes: close-travel settings, debris or a stiff or frozen bottom seal, track or roller resistance, door section movement problems, and opener faults. We don't offer a universal limit-setting procedure because it varies by model.

Why Does My Garage Door Close Completely Then Reopen?

Typically the opener detects something at the floor: a travel setting that asks the door to go too far, unexpected resistance, or a control fault.

Behavior and diagnostics vary by manufacturer. A professional opener assessment looks at travel, force monitoring and controls. Homeowners should not override reverse-protection settings.

Why Won't My Garage Door Close With the Remote or Wall Button?

Check the remote first: weak batteries, lost programming, a wall-control lock or vacation mode, or a sensor interruption can each stop a close command.

If the wall button works but the remote does not, the remote or its signal is likelier. If neither works, suspect a sensor, power or the opener. Some doors close only while a control is held down. That is the opener signaling a safety fault, not a fix, and holding the button to defeat it is unsafe. Behavior varies by manufacturer, so check your manual.

Why Are My Garage Door Opener Lights Flashing?

Flashing lights or arrows are a diagnostic signal. They can point to a sensor, wiring, obstruction or control-board issue.

LiftMaster, Chamberlain, Genie, Craftsman and other systems use different patterns, and some newer models display codes, so we don't give universal flash counts. Your manual explains what yours means, and a technician can diagnose it.

Can Tracks and Rollers Prevent a Garage Door From Closing?

Yes. A bent track, a misaligned curve or a worn roller creates resistance that stops the door or makes the opener reverse.

Vertical tracks, curved transitions, horizontal tracks, roller bearings, hinges and brackets all affect downward travel. We don't provide track adjustment or roller-resetting steps.

Can Spring or Cable Problems Stop a Garage Door From Closing?

They can contribute, but they are not the cause of every closing failure. Torsion or extension springs, lift cables and drums keep the door balanced, and a fault can make movement abnormal or unsafe.

Signs include uneven lowering, a crooked door, or a door that is unusually heavy or fast. We don't provide spring or cable tensioning instructions.

Why Does One Side of My Garage Door Close Before the Other?

Uneven cable movement, a cable drum problem, roller binding, track alignment or section positioning can lower one side first.

A door hanging crooked may be unstable. Stop operating it rather than forcing it into position, and keep people and vehicles clear.

What Can You Safely Check When Your Garage Door Won't Close?

Observations only, done while the door is stationary.

  • Look for objects in the door's path and at the threshold.
  • Look for an obvious obstruction in front of either photo eye.
  • See whether the sensor lights look normal, using your manual.
  • Check the remote battery and whether the wall control responds.
  • Note any blinking opener indicators or codes.
  • Notice whether the door is crooked, and look from a distance for displaced cables or rollers.
  • In winter, notice whether the bottom seal looks frozen to the floor. Don't pry it.
  • Stop using the door if its movement is unstable.

Do not

  • Bypass or disable photo eyes.
  • Hold controls to defeat safety protection.
  • Increase opener force.
  • Adjust springs, cables or drums.
  • Pull a crooked door closed or force rollers into tracks.
  • Remove bottom fixtures or open energized electrical covers.

How Can Garage Door Opener Travel Settings Affect Closing?

Close-travel limits tell the opener where the floor is, and force monitoring tells it when to reverse. If either is off, the door can stop early or reopen.

Opener force should not be raised to push through a mechanically defective door; that strains the opener and weakens safety protection. Programming differs by manufacturer, so adjustment is part of professional diagnosis.

Garage Door Not Closing FAQs

Why won't my garage door close?

The most common causes are a blocked, dirty or misaligned photo-eye sensor, opener control or power problems, close-travel settings, and mechanical binding in the rollers or tracks. What the door does when you press the button (nothing, reverses, or stops) narrows the cause. A technician can confirm it.

Why does my garage door start closing and then go back up?

Usually the opener is reversing on purpose. A photo-eye interruption, an object in the path, or resistance from binding rollers or tracks can trigger it. Reversal is a safety function, so it should be diagnosed, not defeated.

Why won't my garage door close all the way?

Possible causes are close-travel settings, an obstruction or debris at the threshold, a stiff or frozen bottom seal, roller or track resistance, or an opener fault. Seeing where the door stops helps a technician narrow it down.

Why does my garage door stop a few inches above the floor?

The opener may think the door has reached the floor, something may be blocking it, or resistance may be tripping reversal. A seal frozen or stuck at the floor in winter can also play a part. Do not force the door down.

Why does my garage door close and immediately reopen?

Often the opener's travel or force monitoring is reacting at the floor. Other possibilities include a sensor fault or mechanical resistance. Opener behavior varies by model, so a technician should assess it. Don't override reverse protection.

Can misaligned sensors prevent my garage door from closing?

Yes. The photo eyes must see each other across the opening. If one is bumped, dirty, blocked or has lost power, the opener may refuse to close or may reverse. They should never be bypassed.

How do I know whether a garage door safety sensor is malfunctioning?

Common signs are indicator lights that are off, blinking or different from each other, and a door that won't close with the remote. Check your opener's manual for what its lights mean. Beyond observing, leave testing and repair to a technician.

Why are my garage door opener lights flashing?

Flashing lights are a diagnostic signal. They can point to a sensor, wiring, obstruction or control-board issue, but the meaning differs among LiftMaster, Chamberlain, Genie, Craftsman and other models. Refer to your manual or call for diagnosis.

Why won't my garage door close with the remote?

Check whether the remote battery is weak, whether the remote needs reprogramming, and whether a wall control lock or vacation mode is on. If the wall button works but the remote doesn't, the remote is the likelier fault. If neither works, suspect sensors or power.

Why won't my garage door close using the wall button?

The wall control, its wiring, the opener, a sensor interruption or a drive problem could all be responsible. Behavior when the button is held varies by manufacturer. Don't use hold-to-close to get around a safety fault.

Can worn rollers prevent a garage door from closing?

Yes. Worn or damaged rollers can bind in the track, creating resistance that makes the door stop or the opener reverse. Replacement is best left to a technician because the hardware is near loaded components.

Can track problems cause a garage door to reverse?

Yes. A bent, loose or misaligned track can create resistance that the opener reads as an obstruction. A technician checks the vertical, curved and horizontal sections.

Can broken cables cause uneven closing?

They can. A frayed, slack or displaced cable can make one side lower faster than the other, leaving the door crooked. Stop using the door and don't touch the cable, which is under tension.

Can a spring problem prevent a garage door from closing?

It can contribute. A failed or unbalanced counterbalance spring makes door movement abnormal and sometimes unsafe, but it isn't the cause of every closing failure. Springs are high-tension parts and need a professional.

What is the difference between opener travel settings and door balance?

Travel settings tell the opener where the door's open and closed positions are. Balance is how well the springs offset the door's weight. A balance fault can make a door heavy or fast, and an opener should not be set to compensate for it.

Is it safe to force a garage door closed?

No. Forcing it, or holding controls to defeat safety protection, can cause injury or damage. If the door is crooked, binding or unstable, keep people and vehicles clear and call a professional.

Does a garage door that won't close need a new opener?

Not necessarily. Many closing problems come from sensors, wiring, travel behavior, rollers or tracks. A new opener is worth considering only when diagnosis shows the opener itself has failed or can't be repaired.

Whether the cause is a sensor, opener, wiring or mechanical part; the parts needed; door type; residential or commercial equipment; and the complexity of the work. KSE Door Mechanics doesn't quote prices without inspecting the door.
