How Does a Garage Door Extension Spring System Work?

Extension springs usually sit beside the horizontal tracks on both sides of the door. As the door closes, they stretch and store energy. As it opens, they contract and release it, which helps lift the door.

Each spring works with a pulley and a lift cable. The cable runs from the bottom of the door, around the pulley and back to a fixed point, so the spring’s pull is passed to the door. Brackets and hangers hold the system in place. Many systems also use a safety containment cable through the spring.

Springs versus the opener: the springs counterbalance the weight of the door, and the opener moves the door. A broken spring leaves the opener to lift a heavy door it was not built to carry alone.

Signs Your Garage Door Extension Spring May Be Broken

These signs do not always prove an extension spring has failed. Cables, pulleys, tracks and openers can cause similar symptoms, so a professional should confirm the cause.

Visible separation or broken coils

A gap or separated coil in a spring is a clear sign that it has failed.

Door is hard to lift

A door that feels heavy by hand has lost counterbalance.

Door opens unevenly

One side higher than the other suggests uneven spring tension or a cable issue.

Door stops partway

The door may stall when the opener cannot carry the weight.

A sudden loud snap

A loud bang from the garage can mean a spring or cable has let go.

Changed spring shape

Stretched, bowed or distorted springs may no longer balance the door.

Damaged or displaced pulleys

Wobbling, bent or shifted pulleys can disturb the whole system.

Lift cable irregularities

Fraying, slack or a cable out of position affects lift.

Unusual noise

Scraping, squealing or banging during movement points to worn parts.

Opener struggling

Straining, humming or stopping suggests the springs are not helping enough.

Do not keep operating a suspect door

If you suspect a spring or cable failure, stop using the door. Do not try to lift it, release tension or handle the spring or cables. Call a professional.

Types of Garage Door Extension Spring Systems

Extension spring systems are used on sectional doors. Springs differ in design and load specification, and the hardware around them varies by manufacturer and door model. Paired springs are common, and some systems add pulleys and containment cables.

The safety containment cable is a separate safety component, not a special type of spring. Springs are also rated in operating cycles, so cycle life is one factor in how long a spring lasts.

Common Causes of Extension Spring Failure

Causes should be diagnosed, not assumed. Springs do not all fail for the same reason, and there is no fixed lifespan that applies to every spring.

Metal fatigue

Springs flex with every cycle, and metal wears over time.

Repeated cycles

Busy doors use up a spring’s rated cycles sooner.

Corrosion

Moisture and road salt can weaken steel springs and hardware.

Mechanical wear

Worn pulleys, brackets or cables add strain.

Wrong components

A spring that does not match the door can overwork and fail early.

Pulley or cable problems

Misaligned or frayed parts can load one spring more than the other.

Excessive loading

Added weight, such as insulation or windows, can change the balance needed.

Unbalanced door

A door that is out of balance puts uneven stress on the springs.

Deteriorated attachments

Worn anchors and brackets can fail or let the spring shift.

Why Pulleys and Lift Cables Matter

Pulleys and cables carry the springs’ force to the door. Movable pulleys sit at the spring ends, fixed pulleys at the track side, and spring attachment points and track support hardware hold everything in position. Worn or misaligned parts change how evenly the door lifts.

Warning signs: frayed cables, damaged pulleys, irregular movement, unusual noise, or a spring or cable that has shifted out of position. Arrangements vary by manufacturer. Do not try to remove a loaded cable or pulley yourself.

What Are Garage Door Extension Spring Safety Cables?

A safety cable is a steel cable threaded through the center of an extension spring and anchored at both ends. If the spring breaks, the cable helps hold the pieces so they do not fly across the garage.

Why they are used

Extension springs store a lot of energy. A cable through the spring helps restrain it if it fails.

Why routing matters

The cable must be installed and routed correctly to do its job.

Why to inspect them

Existing containment cables can wear, rust or come loose, and should be checked with the springs.

Missing or damaged

If a safety cable is missing or damaged, have a professional look at it.

Safety cables reduce risk, they do not remove it

A containment cable does not make it safe to handle a spring. Please leave installation and repair to trained professionals.

Why Correct Extension Spring Selection Matters

Springs that look alike are not necessarily interchangeable. A technician considers:

Extension Spring Replacement FAQs

What is a garage door extension spring?

A garage door extension spring is a long coil spring that stretches and contracts as the door moves, helping to counterbalance the door’s weight. Extension springs come in pairs on many sectional doors, with one on each side. They are matched to your door’s weight and are not the same as a general hardware-store spring.

Where are garage door extension springs located?

Extension springs usually run alongside the horizontal tracks on both sides of the door, above the door when it is open. Each spring connects to a pulley and a lift cable. Torsion springs, by comparison, sit on a bar above the door opening. If your springs run parallel to the tracks, you likely have an extension system.

How do extension springs work?

As the door closes, the springs stretch and store energy. As it opens, they contract and release that energy, helping lift the door through pulleys and cables. This is why a door can feel light when balanced. The opener only moves the door; the springs carry most of its weight.

How do I know if an extension spring is broken?

Common signs are a visibly separated or stretched spring, a loud snap, a door that is suddenly heavy or lifts unevenly, or an opener that strains. These signs can also point to cable or pulley problems, so a professional inspection is the reliable way to confirm what failed. Do not keep running a door you suspect is damaged.

Is it safe to open a door with a broken extension spring?

No. A door with a broken spring is much heavier than normal and may drop, bind or lift unevenly. Forcing it can damage the door and opener or cause injury. Please stop using it and call (833) 877-2561. Do not try to release or handle the spring or cables yourself.

Can a broken extension spring be repaired?

A fractured spring generally needs to be replaced, not repaired. Makeshift fixes such as splicing or welding are not safe. Some problems that feel like spring failure are actually caused by cables, pulleys or other hardware, which is why we inspect the whole system. See garage door spring replacement.

Should both extension springs be replaced?

Often it is worth considering. The two springs have worked together for the same number of cycles, and mismatched springs can cause uneven lift. We decide after inspecting spring condition, matching specifications, door balance and the surrounding hardware, not by assuming either way.

What is the difference between torsion and extension springs?

Extension springs stretch alongside the horizontal tracks. Torsion springs twist on a shaft above the door. They use different hardware and replacement parts, and one is not simply swapped for the other. See torsion spring replacement and the comparison table on this page.

What is an extension spring safety cable?

A safety cable is a separate steel cable threaded through the center of an extension spring and anchored at both ends. If the spring breaks, the cable helps contain the pieces so they do not whip around. It reduces risk but does not eliminate it. A missing or damaged safety cable should be checked by a professional.

Why do garage door extension spring pulleys wear out?

Pulleys turn every time the door moves, so their bearings and wheels wear over time. Corrosion, dirt, misalignment and worn or frayed cables speed this up. A damaged pulley can make the door move unevenly or put extra strain on the spring. See garage door cable repair.

How is a compatible replacement spring selected?

A technician considers the spring type, door weight, height and travel, spring length and diameter, load rating, and the pulley and attachment hardware. Springs that look alike are not necessarily interchangeable. Using the wrong spring can leave the door too heavy or too light and strain the opener.

How long do garage door extension springs last?

There is no single lifespan for every spring. Life depends on the spring’s cycle rating, how often the door is used, corrosion, balance and hardware condition. Springs are commonly rated in operating cycles, not years. We can inspect yours and discuss what we find.

Can extension springs be converted to torsion springs?

In some cases a door can be converted, but it needs a professional evaluation, compatible hardware and the right components for the door. It is not a simple spring swap. See spring conversion or call us to discuss whether it suits your door.

Cost depends on the spring specifications, door weight, number of springs, pulley and cable condition, safety cable hardware, parts availability and the overall scope of work.
