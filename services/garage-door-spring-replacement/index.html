Signs You May Need Garage Door Spring Replacement

Quick answer: The most common signs of a broken spring are a gap in the coil, a very heavy door, a loud bang, a door that opens only a few inches, uneven movement and an opener that strains. Several of these can have other causes, so a professional should confirm.

If you suspect a broken spring, don't keep trying the door. A door with no counterbalance can be extremely heavy and may drop without warning.

Visible gap in a torsion spring

A clear separation in the coil usually means the spring has broken.

Door suddenly very heavy

Springs carry most of the door's weight, so losing one makes lifting hard.

Door will not open

The opener may hum or strain without lifting.

Rises only a short distance

The door may open a few inches, then stop.

Loud bang from the garage

A sharp noise can mark a spring snapping.

Uneven movement

One side may rise or sit lower than the other.

Opener strains

The motor is fighting dead weight.

Visible wear or rust

Corroded or stretched coils may be near the end of life.

Door won't stay balanced

It drifts or drops instead of holding its position.

Change in how it moves or sounds

New creaks, pops or jerky travel are worth a call.

Is It the Spring, the Opener, the Cable or the Track?

Quick answer: A broken spring usually shows as a gap in the coil, loose cables and a door that is very heavy to lift. If the remote fails but the wall button works, or the door reverses on closing, the cause is more likely the opener or sensors.

Types of Garage Door Springs

Quick answer: Most residential doors use torsion springs mounted above the door or extension springs along the side tracks. Both counterbalance the door, but they have different hardware, failure signs and safety needs.

Torsion Springs

  • Usually mounted on a shaft above the door
  • Twist to counterbalance the door's weight
  • Single or multiple-spring configurations
  • Work with torsion shaft, cable drums and bearings
  • Failure sign: visible gap in the coil, heavy door
  • Professional inspection checks spring, shaft, drums and cables

Torsion spring replacement

Extension Springs

  • Usually run along the horizontal tracks
  • Stretch to assist lifting
  • Work with pulleys and lift cables
  • Safety containment cables are important
  • Failure signs: stretching, rust, uneven lift, a snap
  • Replacement should match the door and be done professionally

Torsion Springs vs Extension Springs

Quick answer: Torsion springs sit above the door on a shaft and twist, while extension springs run along the tracks and stretch. Which suits your door depends on its design and manufacturer, and not every door can be converted from one to the other.

Common Causes of Garage Door Spring Failure

Quick answer: Springs wear out with use. Repeated cycles, metal fatigue, corrosion, a poorly balanced door, incorrect spring selection and worn related hardware are common causes. Life varies by design, use and maintenance, so no exact figure applies to every spring.

  • Repeated operating cycles
  • Metal fatigue
  • Corrosion
  • General mechanical wear
  • Incorrect spring selection
  • Excessive mechanical loading
  • Poor door balance
  • Related hardware problems

How Technicians Diagnose Spring Problems

We look at the whole counterbalance system before choosing parts, because a fault in one component can mimic or cause another. Inspection covers:

  • Torsion or extension spring type
  • Visible spring condition
  • Counterbalance configuration
  • Door size and weight requirements
  • Shaft condition, bearings and cable drums
  • Lifting cables
  • Track and roller movement
  • Door balance and opener operation
  • Manufacturer compatibility requirements

Why Correct Spring Selection Matters

Quick answer: The right spring is matched to the door's weight, height and counterbalance setup. A spring that only looks similar can leave a door too heavy, too light or unbalanced and strain the opener, cables and tracks.

Professionals consider spring type, wire diameter, inside diameter, length, winding direction where it applies, quantity, door weight and height, drum configuration and cycle rating. Standard springs are rated for a limited number of cycles, and higher-cycle options exist for doors used often.

This is why we don't publish DIY measuring or winding steps. Retail springs sold by size alone may not suit your door.

Why Springs, Cables and Drums Should Be Evaluated Together

Springs, lift cables, cable drums, the torsion shaft, bearings, rollers and tracks all carry or guide the door's load. A worn cable, a seized bearing or a bent track can add strain to a new spring, or make a spring problem look worse than it is.

Can a Broken Garage Door Spring Be Repaired?

Quick answer: A fully broken spring is generally replaced rather than reconnected. We don't splice, weld or improvise spring fixes, because the spring must restore proper counterbalance and safe operation.

Some apparent spring problems turn out to be cables, drums or door imbalance, so diagnosis determines the remedy.

Garage Door Spring Replacement FAQs

How do I know if my garage door spring is broken?

Common signs are a visible gap in a torsion spring, a loud bang, a door that feels very heavy or lifts only a few inches, uneven movement, slack cables, or an opener that strains. These can also come from other parts, so a technician should confirm the cause.

Can I open a garage door with a broken spring?

We recommend against it. The door may be extremely heavy, and forcing it can drop it suddenly or damage the opener, cables and tracks. Keep people and vehicles clear and call for service instead of repeatedly trying the door.

What is the difference between torsion and extension springs?

Torsion springs mount on a shaft above the door and twist to counterbalance its weight. Extension springs run along the horizontal tracks and stretch and contract. Which your door uses depends on its design and manufacturer.

Can a broken garage door spring be repaired?

A fully broken spring is normally replaced, not reconnected. We don't splice, weld or improvise fixes. Some problems that look like spring failure involve cables, drums or balance, so diagnosis comes first.

Should both springs be replaced at the same time?

Often it's worth considering, because both springs in a pair have had the same use, but it isn't required in every case. A technician should check the age, condition, specifications and balance before advising.

Why did my garage door spring break?

Springs wear with each open-and-close cycle. Metal fatigue, corrosion, a poorly balanced door, incorrect spring selection and worn related hardware can all shorten life. Frequent use is another factor.

How long do garage door springs normally last?

It varies with spring design, cycle rating, how often the door is used and maintenance. Standard residential springs are often rated for a limited number of cycles and higher-cycle options exist. We don't promise a specific lifespan.

Can the wrong spring damage a garage door?

Yes. A spring that doesn't match the door's weight can leave it too heavy, too light or unbalanced, and can strain the opener, cables and tracks. Similar-looking springs are not always interchangeable.

Does a new spring affect garage door opener operation?

A correctly balanced door lets the opener work as designed, while a broken or mismatched spring forces the opener to lift dead weight. We check balance and opener operation after replacement.

Do all garage doors use torsion springs?

No. Many residential doors use torsion springs above the door, while others use extension springs along the tracks. Some manufacturers use distinctive counterbalance systems, so identification comes first.

What happens during professional spring replacement?

A technician identifies the spring system, finds what failed, checks related hardware and door weight, selects compatible springs, replaces them with proper tools and safety practices, then tests balance, movement and opener safety.

How much does garage door spring replacement cost?

Cost depends on spring type, size and specification, door weight, number of springs, counterbalance setup, related hardware and service scope. We don't publish a fixed price. Call for an estimate, and ask for it itemized.

Can commercial overhead door springs be replaced?

Yes, where the door and system are within our commercial scope. Commercial doors may use higher-cycle or heavier springs. See our commercial spring page, or call to confirm your door.

Is it worth fixing a broken spring or should I replace the door?

If the door is otherwise sound, replacing the spring is usually the focused fix. A new door may make more sense when many parts are worn or damaged. A technician can help you compare.

Can I replace a garage door spring myself?

We don't recommend it. Springs hold high tension, and incorrect handling can cause serious injury. Correct sizing also matters. We don't publish DIY winding or removal steps.
