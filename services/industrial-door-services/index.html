Types of Industrial Doors

Explanations below are manufacturer-independent. We confirm your specific make and model before scheduling.

Sectional Industrial Overhead Doors

Horizontal panels on heavy-duty hinges travel along vertical and horizontal tracks on rollers, balanced by counterbalance springs and usually driven by a commercial operator. Common in warehouse loading bays. Typical service needs include roller, hinge, track, spring, cable and operator work.

Rolling Steel Industrial Doors

A steel slat curtain coils above the opening, guided by side guides and balanced by a counterbalance assembly. Operation may be motorized or chain-hoist. Service often involves the curtain and slats, guides, counterbalance components and the operator.

Common Industrial Overhead Door Problems

Recognize the symptom, then involve a professional for anything involving springs, cables or powered equipment.

Door will not open

Symptoms
Operator hums or runs with no travel, or no response
Possible causes
Power or control fault, failed operator, broken spring, cable off drum, disengaged disconnect
Operational impact
Blocked loading and access
Safe next step
Keep people clear and stop attempting to run it; request inspection.

Door will not close

Symptoms
Reverses, stops short, or stays open
Possible causes
Misaligned or blocked photo-eyes, safety edge fault, limit settings, obstructed track
Operational impact
Open bay, security and climate exposure
Safe next step
Check for visible obstructions only; have safety devices inspected.

Door moves unevenly

Symptoms
One side lags, door racks or tilts
Possible causes
Cable wear, drum problem, spring imbalance, bent track
Operational impact
Binding, off-track risk, component damage
Safe next step
Stop use and request a professional inspection.

Broken spring

Symptoms
Loud bang, door heavy, operator strains
Possible causes
Fatigue from use, corrosion, wrong spring for the door
Operational impact
Door may be unsafe to operate
Safe next step
Do not operate or adjust; call for replacement and balancing.

Worn or broken cables

Symptoms
Fraying, slack cable, uneven travel
Possible causes
Wear, corrosion, drum misalignment, imbalance
Operational impact
Sudden drop or jamming risk
Safe next step
Keep clear; cables can be under tension. Request professional service.

Failed door operator

Symptoms
Motor runs without moving door, grinding, intermittent control
Possible causes
Motor or gearbox wear, drive fault, control or power fault
Operational impact
Door works only manually, if at all
Safe next step
Confirm the manual provision works safely; request operator diagnosis.

Track misalignment

Symptoms
Door rubs, shakes or jumps off track
Possible causes
Loose or bent track, worn rollers, loose brackets
Operational impact
Repeated derailment, panel damage
Safe next step
Stop use if the door is off track; request inspection.

Roller wear

Symptoms
Grinding, squealing, rough travel
Possible causes
Worn bearings, dry or damaged rollers, track wear
Operational impact
Extra load on operator and hardware
Safe next step
Request inspection; rollers must match the door.

Unusual operating noise

Symptoms
Grinding, scraping, popping, squeal
Possible causes
Dry hardware, worn gears or bearings, loose fasteners
Operational impact
Often an early warning of wear
Safe next step
Note when it occurs and request inspection.

Faulty safety devices

Symptoms
Door will not close, reverses, sensor light flickers
Possible causes
Dirt, misalignment, damaged wiring or edge
Operational impact
Entrapment protection may be compromised
Safe next step
Never bypass them; request inspection.

Excessive resistance

Symptoms
Hard to move manually, slow travel
Possible causes
Imbalance, binding hardware, drive wear, damaged curtain or panels
Operational impact
Strain on operator and parts
Safe next step
Request balance and operation testing.

Recurring failure

Symptoms
Same fault returns after repair
Possible causes
Underlying wear, wrong components, mismatched hardware or operator
Operational impact
Repeat downtime
Safe next step
Request a full system review to compare repair and replacement.

Industrial Door Operator Repair & Installation

Commercial overhead door operators move the door through motors, gearboxes and drive mechanisms (including chain-hoist arrangements), governed by controls, limit settings and safety devices. Faults can sit in the motor, gearbox, control wiring or limits.

Operator or door? If the motor runs but the door does not lift, or the door is hard to move by hand when the manual provision is used, the cause may be the counterbalance or door mechanism rather than the operator. Diagnosis separates the two.

Never bypass interlocks, sensing devices or entrapment protection. Electrical work is performed only within our actual qualifications and applicable requirements. See commercial door operator repair.

Industrial Door Cable & Drum Service

Steel lifting cables wind onto drums as the door travels. Fraying, corrosion, a cable off the drum, uneven door travel, incorrect tension or drum misalignment all call for inspection and, where needed, replacement.

Cables can be under substantial tension. Stop using the door and keep clear. Do not reset or adjust cables yourself.

Related: commercial door cable replacement.

Industrial Door Spring Repair & Replacement

Industrial torsion springs form the counterbalance that offsets the door’s weight. They wear with every cycle, so frequent-use doors place heavier demands on the system. A broken spring typically means a loud bang, a heavy door and an operator that strains.

Correct component selection depends on the door’s configuration and operating cycles; we do not quote generic cycle ratings or replacement intervals. Replacement and balancing are professional work. See commercial door spring replacement.

Industrial Door Track, Roller & Hardware Repair

Vertical and horizontal tracks, rollers, bearings, hinges, brackets, shaft components and reinforcement hardware keep the door aligned and moving smoothly. Wear shows up as noise, rough travel or derailment.

Hardware must be compatible with the door’s design, weight and operating demand. Replacing parts without finding the cause often leads to repeat failures.

Three paths: 1) repair the existing system, 2) replace specific components or the operator, or 3) replace the complete door. We do not publish unsupported cost estimates; an inspection informs the choice.

Industrial Door Safety & Professional Inspection

Hazards to respect

  • High-tension counterbalance systems and heavy moving assemblies
  • Operator controls and entrapment protection (photo-eyes, safety edges)
  • Door travel paths, workers and forklift traffic
  • Electrical isolation and lockout/tagout, for qualified personnel only

Good practice and references

  • Follow manufacturer instructions and keep inspection records
  • Consult DASMA technical data sheets, applicable OSHA workplace requirements, relevant UL standards and local building and fire codes
  • Requirements differ by product; they are not identical for every door

We do not provide DIY procedures for springs, loaded cables or safety devices, and we make no certification claims on this page. Our services do not include locksmith, lock repair, dent, collision or painting work.

Industrial Door Service FAQs

What types of industrial doors can KSE Door Mechanics service?

We service overhead door systems used in business facilities, including sectional overhead doors, rolling steel doors and related operators and hardware. High-speed doors and other specialist systems are reviewed case by case, so call us with your door type, make and symptoms to confirm what we can service.

What is the difference between commercial and industrial garage doors?

Commercial doors typically serve retail, office and service openings, while industrial doors serve warehouses, plants and distribution facilities, often with larger openings and more frequent cycles. The categories overlap, so what matters is the actual door design, operating demand and environment.

Can an industrial overhead door be repaired?

Often, yes. Faults in the operator, tracks, rollers, springs or cables can usually be addressed by replacing the worn component, provided the door itself is structurally sound and compatible parts are available. A professional inspection determines whether repair or replacement makes sense.

What causes a rolling steel door to stop operating?

Common causes include operator or motor faults, control or limit problems, misaligned guides, damaged slats or curtain, and worn counterbalance components. Because several systems can be involved, inspection is the reliable way to find the actual cause.

Can a commercial overhead door operator be repaired?

Frequently. Motor, gearbox, drive, control and limit faults can often be diagnosed and repaired or the operator replaced. We also check whether the real fault is the operator or the door’s counterbalance, since the two can look similar.

What causes industrial door springs to fail?

Springs wear with use, and corrosion, imbalance or a spring not suited to the door can shorten life. High-use doors put more cycles on the spring system. Springs are under high tension, so do not adjust them yourself; request professional replacement and balancing.

How often should industrial overhead doors be inspected?

There is no single interval for every door. It depends on operating cycles, environment, manufacturer instructions and any facility or code requirements. We can review your doors and suggest an inspection schedule that fits how they are used.

Can worn industrial door cables and drums be replaced?

Yes. Worn, frayed or off-drum cables and damaged drums can be inspected and replaced by a professional. Cables can be under substantial tension, so stop using the door and keep clear rather than attempting a reset.

What causes industrial door rollers and tracks to wear?

Frequent cycling, dry or damaged rollers, worn bearings, loose brackets, misalignment and bent track sections all contribute. Replacing hardware without finding the underlying cause can lead to repeat problems.

When should an industrial door be replaced?

Consider replacement when the door is structurally compromised, parts are hard to source, failures keep recurring or the door no longer fits the facility’s operating demand. Inspection helps compare repairing components against replacing the whole system.

What is a high-speed industrial door?

A high-speed door is a high-performance door, often with a fabric curtain, designed for rapid, frequent opening and closing to support traffic flow. It depends on motor controls and safety sensing. Contact us to confirm service for your model.

Do industrial door safety sensors require inspection?

Yes. Photo-eyes, safety edges and related controls are part of a door’s entrapment protection and should be checked during inspection. Never bypass or disable them; if a sensor is faulty, request service.

Can an industrial door be serviced without replacing the entire system?

Often, yes. Springs, cables, rollers, tracks, hardware and operators can frequently be repaired or replaced individually when the door is sound. We will explain whether a component fix or full replacement is the better option.
