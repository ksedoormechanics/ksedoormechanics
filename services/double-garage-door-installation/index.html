What is double garage door installation?

A double garage door usually means one wide sectional overhead door covering a two-car opening. Installation covers measuring the opening, assembling the panels, mounting tracks and a counterbalance system, connecting the opener and testing safe operation.

It helps to separate the terms. A double-width door is one door across the opening. Two independent single doors are two openings with a center post. A sectional door is built from hinged horizontal panels, so a double garage door is not a one-piece slab. Custom wide-opening configurations also exist and need manufacturer-specific specifications. Who should choose a double garage door?

Two-car attached garages

One wide door suits homes with a single wide opening and side-by-side parking.

Detached garages

A shared entrance can simplify access to a detached two-car building.

Replacing an existing wide door

Homeowners swapping an aging double-width door can often keep the same opening.

Design-led homes

A wide door shapes the front elevation, so panel style and color matter.

Parking layout, vehicle clearance, garage dimensions and architectural preferences all guide the choice.

Common double garage door sizes

Typical nominal sizes are 14×7, 16×7, 16×8 and 18×7 feet, plus other manufacturer-supported sizes. These are examples, not universal standards. Older and custom homes often have non-standard openings, so the real opening, clearance and door system must be measured before ordering.

Door styles and design configurations

Choose from raised-panel, flush-panel, carriage-house and contemporary designs, with or without window sections, in factory-finished colors or wood-look finishes. Insulated and non-insulated configurations are available.

Panel proportions matter on a wide door. Long horizontal panels read as modern, while smaller recessed panels and decorative hardware suit traditional homes. Windows in the top section add daylight without reducing privacy as much as lower glass.

Opening measurements and clearance

Opening width and height

The base for door size and the first thing verified on site.

Headroom

Space above the opening for tracks and springs. Low headroom may need specific track layouts.

Side room and backroom

Room beside the opening for tracks and depth for the door and opener rail.

Jamb, header and floor

Condition, level and mounting support. Wide openings put more load on the header.

Obstructions

Ceiling lights, pipes, framing and opener rail clearance.

Structural modifications require assessment by a qualified professional. A technician verifies fit, and we do not guess.

Insulation, weather protection, wind and structure

Insulation: insulated doors use polystyrene or polyurethane cores. Compare manufacturer-reported R-values for the exact model, and note that perimeter seals, bottom seals and air leakage affect comfort too. This matters most for attached garages. No door can guarantee energy savings. See weatherstripping.

Wind and structure: local building requirements may call for wind-rated configurations with specific reinforcement, mounting and hardware. Not every door suits every wind-load condition, so follow the manufacturer specification and local code. Wide doors need rigidity and solid header support.

Springs, tracks, hardware and opener compatibility

A wide door relies on engineered components: torsion springs (or extension springs where applicable), lift cables, drums, hinges, rollers, tracks, brackets and reinforcement. Counterbalance is sized to the door weight, so heavier doors need correctly engineered parts.

Safety first

Springs and cables hold extreme tension. Never adjust them yourself. We do not provide DIY spring instructions.

Openers: an existing opener may be reusable if its rating, condition and the door's weight and balance match the manufacturer specifications. Rail length, travel, reinforcement, safety sensors and auto-reverse testing all matter. Learn about garage door openers.

Common installation challenges and what a correct install looks like

Measurement errors

Wrong opening dimensions lead to poor fit and gaps.

Headroom and mounting

Insufficient headroom or weak support can limit track options.

Floor and tracks

Uneven floors and wrong tracks cause binding and rubbing.

Spring sizing

Improper spring sizing leaves the door unbalanced.

Opener mismatch

An opener that cannot handle the door leads to strain or false reversals.

Signs of a correct install

Clear drums

Cables seat in drums and drums clear drywall and framing.

Aligned sensors

Photo-eyes are mounted low, typically within about 6 inches of the floor, and aligned.

Even gaps

A small, even gap at the sides, with no rubbing on the jamb.

Smooth travel

The top section seats flush and the door moves quietly.

A professional inspection catches these before they become problems.

Repair, replacement and maintenance

Repair or replace? Weigh door condition, structural integrity, hardware compatibility, age, insulation goals, appearance and opener compatibility. A single worn part may be a repair job, while damaged sections or recurring failures often point to replacement. See garage door repair and garage door replacement.

Maintenance: homeowners can safely watch for worn rollers and hinges, loose hardware, damaged seals, new noises and changes in balance. Test the safety reversal as the manufacturer directs. Leave spring, cable and bracket work to professionals. See maintenance.

Why professional installation matters: accurate sizing, secure mounting, correct counterbalance, reinforcement, track alignment, opener compatibility and safety testing all depend on skilled work that follows manufacturer instructions.

Double garage door FAQs

What is a double garage door?

A double garage door is a wide overhead door, commonly about 16 feet wide, that covers a two-car garage opening. It is usually one sectional door made of horizontal panels that roll up along tracks. It is not one solid piece and it is not two separate doors.

What are common double garage door sizes?

Common nominal sizes include 14×7, 16×7, 16×8 and 18×7 feet, with other manufacturer-supported sizes available. These are examples, not universal standards, so the actual opening and clearances must be measured before a door is ordered.

Is a 16-foot-wide garage door suitable for two cars?

Often yes for two typical passenger vehicles, but it depends on your vehicles, parking layout and how much room you want to open car doors. Larger trucks and SUVs may be more comfortable in an 18-foot opening, if the structure and side room allow it.

Can double garage doors be insulated?

Yes. Many double-width sectional doors are made with polystyrene or polyurethane insulation. Manufacturers report R-values for specific door models, so compare those published figures and the perimeter seals rather than relying on general claims.

What materials are available for a double garage door?

Common choices are steel, insulated steel, aluminum, wood and composite construction. Availability, weight, finish options and hardware requirements vary by manufacturer and model.

Can a double garage door include windows?

Yes. Many styles offer window sections, usually in the top panel. Glass options, wind-rating and insulation performance vary by model, so check the manufacturer specifications for the exact configuration.

Can my existing opener be reused?

Sometimes. It depends on the opener's condition, its rating, and the weight and balance of the new door. A wider door can change the load, so the opener should be checked against the manufacturer's specifications and re-tested after installation.

What measurements are required?

At minimum: opening width and height, headroom above the opening, side room on each side and backroom depth. The jamb and header condition, floor level, ceiling obstructions and opener rail clearance matter too.

How long does double garage door installation take?

It depends on the door, hardware, the condition of the opening and any opener work. Manufacturer DIY guidance often cites around 8 to 10 hours for a double door. A professional crew works faster, and the schedule is confirmed during consultation.

What affects double garage door installation complexity?

Door weight, opening width, headroom, mounting support, floor level, existing hardware, spring and track type, window sections, wind-rated reinforcement and opener integration all affect the work.

What hardware is needed for a wide door?

A wide door typically needs heavy-duty tracks and brackets, correctly sized torsion springs or another engineered counterbalance, lift cables and drums, hinges, rollers and reinforcement such as struts. Exact parts follow the door manufacturer's specification.

How should a double garage door be maintained?

Look over the door a few times a year for worn rollers, loose hardware, frayed cables, damaged seals and changes in sound or balance, and follow the manufacturer's guidance. Leave springs, cables and brackets to a professional.

Is one double door better than two single doors?

Neither is always better. One wide door gives a unified look and one large opening. Two single doors give independent operation and a center post. The best fit depends on the opening, structure, budget and appearance you want.

When should a double garage door be replaced?

Consider replacement when panels are damaged, sections are warped or rotting, hardware is worn or incompatible, repairs keep recurring, or you want insulation, wind-rated construction or a new look. A professional inspection can compare repair and replacement.

How much does double garage door installation cost?

Cost depends on size, material, insulation, windows, wind rating, hardware, opener work and old-door removal. Advertised flat-price specials often exclude these factors, so request a quote based on your actual measurements.

Can I install a double garage door myself?

Manufacturers publish model-specific instructions, but double doors are heavy and use high-tension springs and cables that can cause serious injury. Professional installation is recommended, and spring adjustment should never be a DIY task.
