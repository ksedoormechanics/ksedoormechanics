What Is Garage Door Torsion Spring Replacement?

Torsion spring replacement swaps a failed spring on the shaft above your garage door for a compatible one. These springs counterbalance the door by applying torque through cable drums and lift cables. When one fails, that balance is lost. Replacement needs correct spring selection and controlled handling by a trained technician, followed by checks of balance, alignment and safe operation. How Does a Garage Door Torsion Spring System Work?

The counterbalance system lifts most of the door’s weight mechanically. The opener is separate: it guides the door and supplies motion, but it is not designed to carry the full load alone. Here is how the parts relate.

Torsion spring
Mounted on the shaft above the door. It twists to store and release torque that offsets the door’s weight.
Torsion shaft
The steel tube or bar that carries the spring’s torque across the door opening.
Center bearing or anchor
Holds the spring assembly and shaft in position; on dual-spring systems the springs sit on either side.
End bearings
Let the shaft turn smoothly at each end. Worn bearings add friction and noise.
Cable drums
Wind and unwind the lift cables as the door moves, turning shaft torque into lifting force.
Lift cables
Connect the drums to the door’s bottom fixtures and carry the lifting load.
Bottom fixtures
Attach the cables to the lowest section. They are under load and are not for homeowner handling.
Door sections and opener
The sections ride in the tracks. The opener only guides and moves the door; the springs do the lifting.

Signs Your Garage Door Torsion Spring May Be Broken

Visible separation

A gap in the coil above the door is a clear sign of a broken spring.

Sudden loud bang

A sharp noise from the garage can mean a spring let go.

Door feels heavy

Without counterbalance, the full weight of the door shifts to your arms and the opener.

Door won’t open

The opener runs but the door stays down.

Opens only a few inches

The door lifts slightly, then stops or the opener shuts off.

Opener strains

Humming, straining or slowing during lift can point to lost balance.

Uneven movement

One side rises faster, or the door tilts or jams.

Won’t stay balanced

The door drifts down or won’t hold at the halfway point.

Corrosion or deterioration

Heavy rust or a worn coil can signal a spring near the end of its service life.

Changed performance

The door has gradually become harder to move or less smooth.

Not every symptom proves a broken spring

Cables, drums, tracks and openers can cause similar problems. Get a professional diagnosis, and do not repeatedly operate a door you suspect has a broken torsion spring.

Single vs Dual Garage Door Torsion Spring Systems

Single torsion spring: one spring carries the counterbalance. Common on many lighter or narrower doors. Balance and component compatibility still need to be checked.

Dual torsion springs: two springs share the load. The springs should be matched, and a paired system is inspected as a pair because both have seen the same wear.

Neither design is automatically better; the right setup depends on the door.

Common Causes of Garage Door Torsion Spring Failure

Service life varies with design and use, so we don’t quote a fixed lifespan or cycle count.

Metal fatigue

Springs flex with every cycle, and steel eventually fatigues.

Repeated cycles

Frequent daily use brings wear sooner than occasional use.

General wear

Friction, aging and worn hardware add stress.

Corrosion

Moisture and road salt can roughen and weaken steel.

Unsuitable spring

A spring that doesn’t match the door works harder.

Unbalanced door

Binding tracks, added weight or damaged parts overload the spring.

Excessive loading

Heavier doors, added insulation or accessories can change what the spring must lift.

Related hardware

Worn cables, drums and bearings can add load to the system.

Why Correct Torsion Spring Sizing Matters

A spring that looks similar can behave very differently. Professionals match these factors to the actual door and manufacturer specifications:

  • Wire diameter
  • Inside diameter
  • Spring length
  • Spring design
  • Winding orientation (left- or right-wound)
  • Door weight
  • Door height
  • Drum configuration
  • Shaft compatibility
  • Single or dual spring setup
  • Operating cycle requirements
  • Manufacturer specifications

Parts-store springs are sold by size and winding direction, but fit still depends on the door’s actual weight and setup. We don’t publish winding or turn calculations, because that is tension work for trained technicians.

Why Cables and Drums Matter During Torsion Spring Replacement

Springs, shaft, drums, cables and bearings work as one system. Worn cables, damaged drums, cable misalignment or worn bearings can cause uneven movement and add stress to a new spring.

We inspect these related parts. They are not automatically replaced on every job; they are replaced when their condition calls for it. See garage door cable repair.

Should You Replace One Torsion Spring or Both?

On a dual-spring door, technicians often suggest replacing both, because the surviving spring has done the same number of cycles. That is a common recommendation, not a rule. A technician weighs these factors:

  • Single versus paired system
  • Spring age and operating cycles
  • Matching specifications
  • Visible wear and remaining condition
  • Overall door balance
  • Manufacturer recommendations
  • Safety considerations

A professional assessment gives you a clear reason for either choice.

Torsion Spring Replacement FAQs

What is a garage door torsion spring?

A torsion spring is a tightly wound steel coil mounted on a shaft above the garage door. It twists to store energy and releases torque through the cable drums and lift cables, offsetting the door’s weight so it can be lifted with far less effort.

How does a torsion spring work?

The spring is under torque on the shaft. As the door lowers, the cables unwind from the drums and add tension to the spring. As it rises, the spring releases that torque and helps lift the door. The opener only guides the door; it does not supply this lifting force.

How do I know if my torsion spring is broken?

Look for a visible gap in the coil, a loud bang, a door that feels very heavy or won’t open, or an opener that strains or stops after a few inches. These symptoms can also come from cables, drums or the opener, so a professional should diagnose it.

Can I open my garage door with a broken torsion spring?

It is not advisable. Without its spring, the door is much heavier than the opener is designed to lift, and it can move unpredictably. Repeated attempts can strain the opener and damage parts. Keep people and vehicles clear and call a professional.

What is the difference between torsion and extension springs?

Torsion springs mount on a shaft above the door and work by twisting. Extension springs run along the horizontal tracks and work by stretching. They are different systems with different parts and safety hardware. See our page on extension spring replacement for that design.

Can a broken torsion spring be repaired?

A fractured spring is generally replaced, not repaired. Splicing, welding or improvising a broken spring back into service is unsafe. A technician can confirm the failure, select a compatible replacement and check the related cables, drums and bearings.

Should one or both torsion springs be replaced?

On a dual-spring system, technicians often recommend replacing both because the second spring has seen the same use. It is not an absolute rule. The decision depends on spring age, matching specifications, wear, door balance and manufacturer guidance, so a professional assessment helps.

Why do garage door torsion springs break?

Most springs fail from metal fatigue after many cycles. Corrosion, general wear, an unsuitable spring, an unbalanced door or heavy loading can speed that up. Service life varies with the spring design and how often the door is used.

How long do torsion springs normally last?

There is no single lifespan. It depends on the spring’s design, size, quality, door weight and how many times the door cycles each day. A technician can look at a spring’s condition and compare it with manufacturer specifications if available.

Does spring size affect garage door operation?

Yes. Spring size must match the door’s weight, height, drum and shaft setup. A spring that is too weak or too strong can leave the door heavy, fast-closing or unbalanced. Physically similar springs from a parts store may not be interchangeable.

What happens if the wrong torsion spring is installed?

An incorrect spring can leave the door out of balance, make it hard to lift or likely to drop, overload the opener and wear cables, drums and bearings faster. That is why sizing and compatibility are checked against the actual door before parts are chosen.

Can a broken torsion spring damage an opener?

It can. An opener is built to guide a counterbalanced door, not lift the full weight alone. Running it against a broken spring can strain the motor and drive parts and stress the door. Stop using the opener and call for an inspection.

What does professional torsion spring replacement involve?

In general: an inspection of the spring system and related parts, selection of compatible springs, replacement by a trained technician using suitable tools and safety procedures, and testing of balance, cables, movement and opener operation. Steps vary by door and system.
