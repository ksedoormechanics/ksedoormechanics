How garage door springs work

A sectional door can weigh well over a hundred pounds. Springs store energy and offset that weight through lift cables and cable drums, so an opener or a person only guides the door. When the counterbalance weakens or fails, the door feels heavy, drifts, or loads the opener.

Single or dual springs: many residential doors use one, heavier doors often use two. Dual springs are not universally required. What matters is that the spring matches the door's weight and the manufacturer's requirements. A wrong-size spring can leave a door out of balance and load the opener.

Why we do not tell you to lift the door by hand

Some online guides suggest disconnecting the opener and lifting the door to test balance. If a spring has failed, the door can be much heavier than expected and can drop. We do not recommend manual lift tests or releasing the opener when a spring failure is suspected. Watch from a safe distance and note what you see instead.

What you can safely observe

  • Watch normal operation from a safe distance.
  • Listen for new sounds.
  • Note any visible gap without approaching the spring.
  • Notice uneven movement, heaviness or opener strain.
  • Keep the opening clear and take a photo.

Please do not touch, adjust, wind, disconnect or dismantle springs, cables, drums or brackets.

Cycle life, corrosion and Boston weather

Springs are rated for a number of open-and-close cycles, and standard residential springs are often rated around 10,000 cycles, with longer-life options available. A door used many times a day uses that up faster. These are manufacturer ratings, not guarantees, and rust alone does not set a spring's remaining life.

Boston winters bring moisture, condensation and road salt, and coastal air adds humidity. Those speed corrosion on coils, shafts and hardware. Cold does not automatically break a spring. Before winter and after salt season are sensible times to inspect.

How often to inspect and maintain

An annual professional inspection is a useful starting interval for many homes. Inspect sooner for heavy daily use, old springs, past repairs, visible rust or any change in how the door moves. Follow manufacturer guidance.

Between visits, watch the door's operation and keep notes. Manufacturer-approved lubrication can reduce noise and friction, but it does not repair or prevent failure of a worn spring. See lubrication and maintenance.

Second opinion on a replacement quote

If another company says your springs need replacing, an inspection can document whether they are broken, rusted or stretched, and whether the size matches the door. Springs that are intact and balanced may not need replacement. We explain findings and do not pressure you.

Spring inspection vs safety inspection and tune-up

A spring inspection is one part of a broader check. A garage door safety inspection also covers tracks, rollers, opener safety sensors and automatic reversal. A tune-up adds lubrication and adjustments. Balance can be tested separately through balance testing.

Garage door spring inspection FAQs

What is a garage door spring inspection?

It is a professional check of your spring system: spring type, visible damage or separation, corrosion, mounting and support hardware, the cables and drums, and any balance symptoms. It ends with findings and recommendations. It does not automatically include adjustment, repair or replacement.

What are the signs of a damaged garage door spring?

A loud bang, a visible gap in a torsion spring, a door that feels heavy or will not stay up, a door that opens only a little, slack cables, uneven movement, or an opener that strains. Several of these can also come from cables, rollers, tracks or the opener, so inspection confirms the cause.

Can a broken spring stop a garage door from opening?

Yes. Springs carry most of the door's weight. When one breaks, the opener may lift the door only a short way, strain, or stop. Do not keep pressing the button.

Is it safe to operate a garage door with a broken spring?

No. Stop using it, keep people and pets clear, and call a professional. Do not disconnect the opener or try to lift the door by hand. A door with failed counterbalance can be very heavy and can drop.

How do professionals inspect torsion springs?

They identify the spring configuration, look for separation, corrosion and coil condition, check the shaft, bearing plates and end supports, and review the cables, drums and balance symptoms. Technicians use proper tools and methods. Homeowners should not wind or adjust torsion springs.

Are extension springs inspected differently?

Yes. Extension springs run along the horizontal tracks, so inspection also covers safety containment cables, pulleys, attachment points, stretching and uneven extension. These springs are under tension too and should not be handled by homeowners.

Can a garage door spring fail without warning?

Yes, sometimes. A spring can break suddenly after many cycles. Inspection can find visible wear, rust or hardware concerns, but it cannot predict the remaining life of every spring or prevent every failure.

How often should garage door springs be inspected?

An annual professional inspection is a useful planning interval for many homes. Doors used many times a day, older springs, heavy doors, visible rust or any change in operation may call for sooner. Follow the manufacturer's guidance.

Does spring inspection include balance testing?

Balance is part of what we assess because spring condition and door balance are related, but they are not identical. The scope of any visit depends on what is booked and found. Ask us what your appointment covers.

Can lubrication prevent spring failure?

No. Manufacturer-approved lubrication can reduce friction and noise, but it does not repair a fatigued or broken spring and cannot prevent failure.

Does a spring inspection include spring replacement?

No. Replacement, tension adjustment, conversion, cable or drum work and opener work need a separate assessment and your approval. We explain the findings first so you can decide.

Can cold weather affect garage door springs?

Winter moisture, road salt and condensation can add corrosion and stiffen lubricants. Cold does not automatically break a spring. Inspecting before winter and after salt season is a sensible habit in Boston.

What affects inspection complexity and how long it takes?

Spring type and number, door size and weight, hardware access, existing damage, cable condition, and specialized track systems all matter. We do not promise fixed durations or prices before seeing the door.
