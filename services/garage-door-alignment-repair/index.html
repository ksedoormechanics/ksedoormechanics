What Does Garage Door Alignment Repair Include?

Garage door alignment repair is the process of finding out why a sectional door is moving unevenly, rubbing, binding, or looking crooked, and then choosing the right mechanical correction. A technician checks the tracks, rollers, hinges, brackets, lift cables, drums, and counterbalance before deciding what needs adjusting, repairing, or replacing. Realigning is not always the right fix, because a crooked door is often a symptom of a cable, spring, or hardware problem.

  • Alignment assessment and track position inspection
  • Roller, hinge, and support bracket evaluation
  • Lift cable and counterbalance assessment
  • Door section alignment evaluation
  • Suitable repairs, with compatible parts where needed
  • Operational and safety testing

How Does a Garage Door Stay Properly Aligned?

A sectional door rides on rollers through vertical tracks, a curved transition, and horizontal tracks held by brackets and hangers. Hinges join the panels. Lift cables and drums lift both sides together, and springs offset the door's weight so it moves evenly. The opener only pushes and pulls.

These systems work together, but they do different jobs. Track position guides, balance carries the weight, and the opener controls travel. A fault in one shows up as a symptom in another, which is why a crooked door needs diagnosis, not a guess.

How Track Position Affects Garage Door Alignment

Vertical tracks keep the door plumb, horizontal tracks carry it overhead, and the curve transfers it between the two. Rollers need clearance in every section. Track supports and mounting surfaces hold that position, so a track can be misaligned without being bent. If supports loosen or the mounting surface shifts, the track moves and the door binds.

This is a job for a technician. Tracks carry a heavy moving door, so we do not give measurements or adjustment steps for do-it-yourself work. See garage door track repair.

How Rollers, Hinges and Brackets Affect Alignment

Nylon and steel rollers, roller bearings and stems, end and center hinges, and top fixtures all guide the sections. Worn or mismatched hardware causes binding and irregular movement, and parts are not interchangeable between doors. See roller, hinge, and bracket replacement.

Why Does One Side of My Garage Door Close Before the Other?

The two sides are no longer lifting or lowering equally. Likely causes are a lift cable problem, a cable drum irregularity, a binding roller, track misalignment, section misalignment, or a counterbalance fault. It can signal mechanical instability, so do not push the lower side into place.

Not always the door

Before you call, note what else can look like misalignment: an uneven floor or slab, ice or debris under the door, a worn bottom seal, sunlight or dirt on the safety sensors, or opener travel limits. Mention these when you call.

After a recent repair or install

If the door went crooked after a new spring, cable, or door, say so. Parts that do not match the door's weight or hardware are a common cause. See cable repair, drum replacement, and spring replacement.

Is Your Garage Door Crooked or Actually Off Track?

A crooked door still runs in its track. An off-track door has at least one roller outside it and may be unstable. The two can overlap, and an off-track door should not be used. See off-track garage door repair.

Garage Door Alignment vs Garage Door Balance

Alignment is the position and movement of door components. Balance is how well the springs offset the door's weight. An unbalanced door may move unevenly, and a door can be aligned yet badly balanced, so some symptoms need both checked.

Is It a Garage Door Alignment Problem or a Safety Sensor Problem?

A door that will not close may have a sensor problem, not a mechanical one. Safety sensors are photo-eyes near the floor on each side of the door. If they are blocked, dirty, hit by sunlight, or knocked out of position, the opener may reverse the door. Indicator lights vary by model.

A door that runs straight but reverses before closing points to the sensors or opener. A door that rubs or tilts points to alignment. Never disable or bypass the sensors. See opener services or sensor repair.

Can a Garage Door Opener Cause Uneven Door Movement?

An opener's rail, trolley, motor, travel limits, and force controls can affect how a door starts, stops, and closes. But an opener should not be forced to overcome binding. Raising force to push through a crooked or rubbing door can damage the opener, the door, and the tracks.

If mechanical resistance or a counterbalance problem is the cause, the door must be fixed first.

Is It Safe to Use a Misaligned Garage Door?

Not if it is unstable or binding. A garage door is a heavy moving assembly, and a crooked door may involve a cable or spring problem. Some parts are under high tension, a visibly unstable door can move unexpectedly, and continuing to run a binding door can make the damage worse.

  • Stop using an unstable door and keep children and pets away.
  • Do not stand under a compromised door.
  • Do not force the opener or push rollers into tracks.
  • Do not loosen bottom cable fixtures or adjust spring tension.
  • Contact a qualified garage door professional.

How to Recognize Garage Door Alignment Problems Early

Look at supports from a safe distance, and follow manufacturer-recommended inspections. Garage Door Alignment Repair FAQs

What does garage door alignment repair include?

It starts with an inspection of the door, tracks, rollers, hinges, brackets, lift cables, drums, and counterbalance to find why the door moves unevenly. Repairs may include adjusting, repairing, or replacing the parts at fault, followed by operational and safety testing. The exact work depends on what the inspection finds.

How do I know if my garage door is misaligned?

Common signs are a crooked or tilted door, uneven gaps at the sides or bottom, scraping or grinding, rollers that move irregularly, a door that binds or jerks, and one side sitting lower. Look from a safe distance with the door stationary. Do not run an unstable door to test it.

Why is my garage door crooked?

Usually one side is lifting or lowering differently. Causes include a lift cable or drum problem, a worn or binding roller, a shifted track or bracket, a damaged hinge, or uneven spring balance. A recent spring, cable, or door replacement with mismatched parts can also cause it. A professional inspection finds which.

Why does one side of my garage door close before the other?

That points to an imbalance between the two sides. Lift cable stretch or slippage, a cable drum fault, a binding roller, a misaligned track, or a counterbalance problem can all do it. It can signal mechanical instability, so avoid pushing the low side into place and call for inspection.

Can a garage door be realigned without replacing the tracks?

Often, yes. A track can be out of position because supports have loosened or shifted without the metal being bent. Where tracks are bent, damaged, or repeatedly shift, repair or replacement may be considered. A technician decides after inspecting the door under safe conditions.

What causes garage door track alignment problems?

Loose or shifted track supports, impact damage, worn rollers pushing against the track, hardware that does not suit the door, and settling or movement in the framing can all play a part. Cable and spring faults can also load a track unevenly. Not every crooked door starts with the track.

Can worn rollers cause alignment problems?

Yes. Worn, cracked, or seized rollers can bind in the track, make the door rub, and let a section wander. Rollers must match the door and track. Replacement is a routine mechanical repair. Do not try to push a roller back into a track yourself.

Can damaged hinges make a garage door move unevenly?

Yes. Bent, cracked, or loose hinges change how the sections fold through the curved track, which can cause rubbing and binding. Hinges differ by position and door, so they are not interchangeable. A technician should inspect and replace them.

Can broken cables cause a garage door to become crooked?

Yes. If a lift cable breaks or slips off its drum, one side loses support and the door can tilt or hang unevenly. Cables are under tension. Keep clear, do not lift the door, and call a professional right away.

What is the difference between garage door alignment and balance?

Alignment is about where door parts sit and how they move, such as tracks, rollers, hinges, and sections. Balance is how well the springs offset the door's weight. A door can be well aligned and poorly balanced, and poor balance can make a door move unevenly. Some problems need both checked.

Is an off-track garage door the same as a misaligned garage door?

Not exactly. An off-track door has one or more rollers out of the track and may be unstable. A misaligned door still runs in its track but moves or sits unevenly. They can overlap, and an off-track door should not be used. See our off-track repair page.

Can a garage door opener cause uneven closing?

Sometimes. Opener travel limits, force settings, or a worn trolley can affect how the door closes, and an opener should never be forced to overcome binding. If the door is crooked or rubbing, the cause is usually mechanical. Have the door and opener assessed together.

Is sensor alignment the same as mechanical door alignment?

No. Safety sensors, or photo-eyes, are small units near the floor on each side of the door that detect obstructions. If they are misaligned, the door may reverse or refuse to close, even when the door itself is straight. Never bypass them. See our opener service for sensor problems.

Is it safe to operate a crooked garage door?

Not if it looks unstable, is binding, or has a cable or spring problem. A door that moves unevenly can twist, drop, or damage its tracks and opener. Stop using it, keep children and pets away, do not stand under it, and call a qualified garage door professional.

Does every alignment problem require replacement parts?

No. Some problems are fixed by adjusting or resecuring parts. Others need new rollers, hinges, brackets, cables, or tracks. Replacing the whole door is rarely the first answer. A good inspection explains what is needed and why before you approve work.

Can a misaligned garage door damage its tracks?

Yes. A door that keeps rubbing or binding can wear or bend tracks, damage rollers and hinges, and strain the opener. The longer it is used in that condition, the more parts may be affected. Stopping use and scheduling an inspection helps limit the damage.

What happens during a professional alignment inspection?

A technician checks door position, sections and joints, roller travel, tracks and supports, hinges and brackets, cables and drums, springs, and balance. They also watch opener behavior and safety sensors. You then hear what was found and your repair options before authorizing work.
